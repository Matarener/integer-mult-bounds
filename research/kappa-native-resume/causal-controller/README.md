records cannot be compressed to eleven fields. It establishes no new κ.

The most useful integration development is the **compatible cheaper clean-J

The remaining
join is the changed caller, observations, records, and global hosting.

## 1. A native producer acquisition that costs8H8

backward producer \(B_s\), its selected eight \(X_T\) fields already share
\(F_{s-1}\). Copy them into eight explicitly cleared, newly owned full-cover
records. The producer runs while these records remain unchanged. Each record
then crosses the eight fresh axes through one canonical rank8 child.

After promotion to \(F_s\), two legal query views can be formed:

\[
a=R_T D_s X_{T,\mathrm{pre}},\qquad
q=R_T X_{T,\mathrm{pre}}.
\]

The actual backward source identity identifies a with its current Y query,
\(a=R_sY_{\mathrm{post}}\). At the first F0/B1 pair, q is exactly the old
forward query f. At later stages q is the stated selected-X query; identifying
or converting it to a differently routed prior f is a further consumer task.
The word does not silently count that later identification as free.

Only eight records live across the backward producer. The second eight output
fields are allocated after promotion. All sixteen start with independent dirty

## 5. Why sixteen fixed query records do not compress to eleven

This check retains every owner rather than replacing owner maps by scalar
identities. Work on payload Fourier character zero, where all payload phases
and translations disappear but right owner multiplication remains exact.

Grant the receiver every completed-X output and even free normalization of
the eight selected Y_T fields. Put \(u=x_{\mathrm{pre},U}\),
\(A=R_{s,U}/2\), and \(B=R_{s+1,U}/2\). The selected section makes f and u
independent. Eliminating arbitrary old-Y nuisance from the receiver leaves
only

\[
h=Bu-Zf,
\]

where \(Z=(R_{s+1,U}K_{s,U}-11\ell_{s+1,T}P_{s,T})/2\). Recovering the exact
joint targets f and c therefore has minimum extra linear record rank

\[
\operatorname{rank}([f;Au;h])-\operatorname{rank}(h)
=\operatorname{rank}([A;B]).
\]

This allows arbitrary coding with every source input and arbitrary cross-owner
mixing; it does not assume that eight records remain separately designated f.

Both diagonal Gram blocks are12I. For \(C=AB^*\), all96 relative ordinary-port
owner routes are distinct. Collecting every identical73D owner path gives

\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(CC^*)}{|G|}=96,\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}((CC^*)^2)}{|G|}=2208.
\]

The block Gram spectrum lies in[0,24]. Applying
\(q(t)=1-(1-t/12)^4\), which is zero at0 and lies in[0,1] on that interval,
proves

\[
\frac{\operatorname{rank}([A;B])}{|G|}
\ge16-\frac{2\cdot2208}{12^4}
=\frac{1705}{108}>15.
\]

One full-cover record has only \(|G|\) coordinates on this specialization.
Thus **at least16 full-cover records** are needed for these exact joint
queries, even under the generous receiver. All four actual stage pairs give
the same bound. Collapsing every route to identity reduces the bound to8,
which explains why a scalar specialization can badly underestimate this cost.

This rules out transferring the historical eleven-block endpoint result to
the present fixed f/c interface. That earlier result changed the source
encoding. A changed producer, use of other completed banks, paid access to
ordinary Y holes, different record frames, or a decoder that computes final
corrections without reconstructing both queries can change this bound's target.
