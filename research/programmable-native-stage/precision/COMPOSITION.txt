A compositional sufficient exact-grid theorem for emitted dyadic callers

This applies to a finite, fully specified circuit whose operations are ordinary
monomial words, exact dyadic scalar ADDs, and the source Gaussian children. It
is an arithmetic-format theorem. It does not admit an unimplemented caller or
charge a native child as ordinary work.

Fix positive field-column width f and an initial grid 2^-g Z[i]. For each
original field v set b_v=g. Process the literal circuit in chronological order:

  monomial(v): b_v is unchanged;
  Gaussian(rank r, v): b_v <- b_v + ceil(r f/2);
  ADD(v <- v + c u), c=m/2^t with integer m and t>=0:
      b_v <- max(b_v,b_u+t).

At every prefix, each real and imaginary component of v lies in 2^-b_v Z.
The maximum of all prefix exponents is one sufficient common exact grid.
Negative m, zero coefficients, reused sources, and arbitrary dirty initial
values are allowed. Independent initial values are not replaced by zeros.

Proof by chronological induction. A monomial permutes coordinates and
multiplies by Gaussian units. A scalar ADD sends the sum of two stated lattices
into the lattice with the larger exponent. A rank-r child is the tensor product
of r f one-axis Gaussian transforms. For k independent axes its coefficients
are Gaussian integers divided by 2^ceil(k/2), by the Gaussian-integer lemma in
PROOF.txt. This proves the update and covers intermediate butterfly stages as
well as child outputs. Taking a maximum covers all prefixes. No statistical
error estimate or rounding hypothesis occurs.

The b_v values equal longest dependency-path weights in a directed acyclic
version of the emitted circuit: a Gaussian step has weight ceil(r f/2), a
scalar source edge has weight t, and unchanged-value edges have weight zero.
Consequently, if every path has total Gaussian rank at most R, at most s
Gaussian steps of odd rank, and scalar denominator-exponent sum at most T,
then
                       B = g + ceil(R f/2) + s + T
is sufficient. This deliberately loose bound follows from
sum_j ceil(r_j f/2) <= ceil((sum_j r_j) f/2) + s.
For a fixed finite template, R,s,T are constants independent of f, so B=O(f).
The source-bound verifier supplies the substantially sharper bounds for the
actual whole-K forward and backward words rather than this loose estimate.

Magnitude bookkeeping is also constructive. Attach the polynomial 1 in
u=2^(f/2) to every input field. A monomial leaves it unchanged; a rank-r child
multiplies it by u^r; ADD with coefficient c replaces the target polynomial by
its old polynomial plus |c| times the source polynomial. Every physical prefix
has complex component magnitude at most H times its polynomial evaluated at u,
by the triangle inequality and the exact Gaussian row norm u^r. If all such
polynomials have degree at most d and coefficient sum at most A>=1, then all
prefixes have magnitude at most A H 2^ceil(d f/2). A signed numerator format of

     B + ceil(d f/2) + ceil(log2(A H)) + 4

bits is sufficient with conservative temporary-sum headroom. This bound is
linear in f for a fixed caller and fixed input-height bound. The arithmetic
cost of exact divisions, address maps, data movement and Gaussian evaluation
remains explicit; this theorem does not provide a constant-word or asymptotic
running-time shortcut.
