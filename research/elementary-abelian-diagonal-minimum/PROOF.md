# Exact diagonal minimum over elementary abelian 2-groups

Let \(k\ge 2\) and let \(G=(C_2)^k\) be generated by commuting involutions
\(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_k\). Write \(R=\mathbb F_2[G]\) and identify
\(\gamma_i\) with \(1+z_i\), so \(R=\mathbb F_2[z_1,\ldots,z_k]/(z_1^2,\ldots,z_k^2)\).
Let \(\mathfrak m=(z_1,\ldots,z_k)\). The quotient \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\)
has basis the images of \(z_1,\ldots,z_k\), hence dimension \(k\ge 2\). In
particular \(R\) is not a chain ring: the principal ideals \((z_1)\) and
\((z_2)\) are incomparable.

Every group element is \(g=\prod_{i\in S}\gamma_i=\prod_{i\in S}(1+z_i)\).
Expanding and reducing modulo \(\mathfrak m^2\) gives
\[
g-1\equiv\sum_{i\in S}z_i\pmod{\mathfrak m^2}.
\]
If \(S\) is nonempty this image is nonzero. Write \(\pi\) for the quotient
map \(\mathfrak m\to\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\).

A paid factor on \(n\) registers is
\[
F=I+(g-1)xy^T,
\]
where \(g\in G\setminus\{1\}\) and \(x,y\in\mathbb F_2^n\) satisfy \(y^Tx=1\).
No other address operation is free. An identity diagonal coordinate is a
restored dirty role. The target is \(\operatorname{diag}(h_1,\ldots,h_n)\)
with each \(h_i\in G\), and \(t\) is the number of indices with \(h_i\ne 1\).

**Graded product.** The increment of a paid factor has every entry in
\(\mathfrak m\). If two matrices have entries in \(\mathfrak m\), their
product has entries in \(\mathfrak m^2\). By induction on the number \(L\) of
factors, their product is
\[
P=I+\sum_{j=1}^L A_j+C,
\]
where \(A_j=(g_j-1)x^{(j)}(y^{(j)})^T\) and every entry of \(C\) lies in
\(\mathfrak m^2\). Therefore the \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\)-valued matrix
\(M=\pi(P-I)\) equals \(\sum_j v_j\, x^{(j)}(y^{(j)})^T\) with
\(v_j=\pi(g_j-1)\ne 0\).

**Image bound.** View \(M\) as a linear map
\(\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2^n\otimes(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)\). Then
\[
M(w)=\sum_{j=1}^L (y^{(j)}\cdot w)\,(x^{(j)}\otimes v_j),
\]
so the image is spanned by the \(L\) vectors \(x^{(j)}\otimes v_j\) and
\(\dim\operatorname{im} M\le L\).

**Diagonal count.** If \(P=\operatorname{diag}(h_1,\ldots,h_n)\), then
\(M e_i=e_i\otimes\pi(h_i-1)\). The \(t\) nonzero vectors of this form are
linearly independent, so \(t\le L\).

**Matching construction.** For each \(i\) with \(h_i\ne 1\), the factor
\(I+(h_i-1)e_ie_i^T\) is legal. Increments supported on distinct matrix units
multiply to zero, so these \(t\) factors, in any order, equal the target.
The exact paid-call minimum is therefore \(t\), for every finite \(n\) and
every tuple in \(G^n\).

**What this does not recycle.** The chain-ring column lemma in
`research/bit-cyclic-root-barrier/PROOF.md` assumes
\(R=\mathbb F_2[z]/(z^q)\) and one-dimensional socles of principal ideals.
That hypothesis is false here. The same predecessor records the one-column
witness \(U=(z_1,z_2)^T\), whose image has socle dimension 2, and states
that the witness is not a shorter circuit. The argument above is the circuit
obstruction for this alphabet: mixed labels are allowed, and they still
cannot push the graded image dimension above the factor count.

The rank-\(k=1\) algebra is the \(q=2\) case of that chain-ring theorem and
is excluded from the statement. For \(k\ge 2\), restricting a monochrome
target to one \(C_2\) subgroup does not inherit the \(q=2\) theorem, because
the legal alphabet also contains labels outside that subgroup. Those labels
are included in the span above.

**Boundary.** For \(C_4=\langle\gamma\rangle\), the involution is
\(1+z^2\in 1+\mathfrak m^2\), so its image under \(\pi\) is zero. The graded
count does not force four involution calls. The chain-ring socle argument
remains the reason those calls cannot be saved. This note does not recompute
that census and does not change the selected multiplication exponent.
