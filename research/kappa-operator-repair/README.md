## Why the old claim failed

The issue is algebraic. Geometric checks can establish dimensions, orthogonality, legal frames and disjoint placement while missing the effect of moving a phase transformation across a scalar controller that mixes streams. The placement can satisfy those geometric checks and still compute a different linear map.

The inherited witness against the actual submitted move file changes ten entries at owner 0, port 0. This round goes further: it reconstructs the complete signed eight-stream scalar network, including its final translation and global phase. It normalizes the network as

\[
P_t=D_tM_tD_{\mathrm{initial}}^{-1},
\]

where the diagonal factors carry the actual Fourier phases. The baseline proof checks **10,416 exact identities over phase polynomials modulo four**, covering all **112 symbolic ports**. The arbitrary-dirty operator is checked, so cancellation is not inferred from a few convenient input vectors.

For the first faulty group, six literal moves involve five independent active character forms. The exact checker finds a failure on **31 of the 32 binary active assignments**. It also exhausts the generated phase group needed for arbitrary positive tensor multiplicities: **1,008 of 1,024 states are nonidentity**. An independently implemented dense calculation agrees on 19 diagnostic characters. One failed group suffices to reject the whole move file; the checker does not need to spend time enumerating the remaining 82,482 groups after rejection.

The positive T360 gate is separately bound to the new construction. It scans all **1,321,920 hosted helper rows**, uses the actual current helper widths, checks each observer-free interval at the scalar-event level, and verifies distinct original child axes. Within those intervals the hosted factors can be commuted to their original child endpoints and combined with the complementary idle factor. The full child is therefore recovered before applying the symbolic eight-stream controller identity. Old replica counts, numerical profiles and bank padding are not borrowed from the historical template proof.

The guard rejects a missing operator certificate, a geometry-only certificate, finite-only character coverage, an undisclosed phase-axis transfer overlay, a different candidate word, a different native source, an incomplete release, and a supplier root reported without the outer loss. A historical `PASS` string or a correct moment calculation is insufficient.

## Why repairing the retracted move file is not the useful local lever

The counterexample does admit an exact inverse correction. It factors into five grouped completed-phase updates, with child-rank profile `{1:2, 3:2, 4:1}`. This is a useful algebraic construction, but its complete price loses.

There is a sharper obstruction within the retained interface. At an explicit character, the operator defect has rank five: a displayed 5-by-5 minor has determinant **−8i**. Adding one completed single-stream Fourier phase child changes a fixed-character operator by rank at most one. A telescoping rank bound therefore requires at least five added children, even when invertible routing within the character block is granted free.

At `a = 0.001745`, the literal five-factor correction costs about **11.9788439** in raw moment units. An intentionally generous bound puts the entire group’s possible residual repacking gain below **0.6674625**. Even one unit-priced additional child cannot be funded by that group’s maximum gain. This rules out charging the correction as extra ordinary children in the stated model.

The limitation is precise: it covers completed single-stream phase insertions at caller-level cuts, with the original local/hosted bill and residual mass retained. It does not cover a new coupled query that absorbs the correction, adapters mixing Fourier characters, or a different scalar/controller topology.

A separate Gaussian calculation extends the existing parity obstruction to a declared same-phase-slot family with odd Gaussian bridge coefficients. The proof uses reduction by `1−i` and a second-order invariant. Every changed bridge must still normalize into the same 20 phase insertions and a scalar controller ending at the identity. Dyadic denominators, new phase slots and different topologies remain outside that theorem.

## Broader geometries and bank interfaces tested

### A paired phase identity with enough potential saving

The strongest architectural lead is an exact replacement of two rank-four transforms by two rank-two transforms plus a coupled correction. For character bits `x,y,z,w`, retain axes `1110` and `0001`. The missing Fourier sign is

\[
(-1)^{xy+xz+yz}=(-1)^{(x\oplus y)(y\oplus z)+y}.
\]

Set `a=x xor y` and `b=y xor z`. A dyadic two-bank Hadamard turns a translation on one bank into a complementary bank operation. Their commutator obeys

\[
X_a Z_b X_a Z_b=(-1)^{ab}I_2.
\]

A common translation supplies the remaining sign. Both banks may contain arbitrary dirty data. Exact checks cover every coefficient input at one tensor factor, every character at one, two and three factors, and all 512 native columns of the actual quartet residual, including its Gaussian global phase. The algebraic tensor identity extends to every positive multiplicity.

The uncorrected rank reduction would have enough arithmetic saving to beat 0.001745 on the complex side. It computes the wrong operator. The displayed exact fixed-two-bank correction needs **8f bulk additions, 4f scales and 4f+2 translations** at tensor multiplicity `f`. Its bulk work grows with `f`, so it does not provide the required uniform linear tariff.

There is a uniform implementation through ordinary recursive children, but its bill is

\[
2H_4\longrightarrow4H_2,
\]

which is strictly worse for positive `a`. The correction is diagonal in Fourier coordinates. In coefficient coordinates it has `4^f` nonzero entries per row, each of magnitude `2^-f`. It therefore cannot be replaced by a coefficient-wise sign scan or a monomial permutation. This is an obstruction to those proposed implementations, not a general circuit lower bound.

The remaining problem is a uniform coupled implementation whose full bill is favorable, or a changed production/observation macro that absorbs the correction into work already required. The package supplies an exact identity and a losing paid implementation to define that problem concretely.

## What remains, expressed as a payable target

For a complete child histogram `A_r`, define the raw target excess

\[
E(a)=\sum_r A_r r^{1-a}-Wm^{1-a}.
\]

For the new complete complex construction at `a = 0.001745`, exact interval arithmetic gives

\[
E(a)\approx929.74723064957036,
\qquad E(a)/3240\approx0.28695902180542295.
\]

That is the saving required per local call, with the existing complete owner bill fixed. One receiver-type `H1+H2−H3` saving at the same target is about **0.00332810480927729**. Closing the gap would require about **86.223** independent equivalents per local call. This is a budget comparison, not evidence that 87 compatible rewrites exist.

The normalized price convention used by the moment code multiplies those raw quantities by `73^a`: the global excess is **936.73423299546319** and the per-call excess is **0.28911550401094543**. The two conventions agree exactly after this conversion. The earlier paired-phase experiment was priced against the first repaired checkpoint; its proof that the paid `2H4→4H2` correction loses holds for every positive exponent and does not depend on that checkpoint's numerical gap.

The evidence supports three research directions:

1. **Production and observation changes that narrow useful helpers and repack their freed width.** Literal staged entries, valid receiver identities and complete owner fills have already produced real gains. Further changes need simultaneous arbitrary-dirty semantics, frame feasibility and owner pricing. A smaller helper width alone is not a gain certificate.
2. **Coupled phase macros with a uniform full bill.** The paired identity shows the scale of a potentially sufficient saving. The key obligation is to absorb or implement its Fourier-quadratic correction at a cost that leaves a net benefit. The current commutator and ordinary-child implementations do not meet that obligation.
3. **A further BIT improvement once the complex side advances.** The retained BIT lower endpoint is still below the target. Both suppliers must clear the requested regime; improving only the current complex bottleneck cannot prove κ greater than 0.001745 indefinitely.

Repeated phase-axis transfers, optimistic rank-only bills, coefficient-sign substitutes for Fourier corrections, and protected-output relaxations have explicit failures in this package. They should not be reused as evidence of a feasible supplier. The finite basis and word screens instead delimit the particular families already examined, leaving genuinely different production graphs and interfaces open.
