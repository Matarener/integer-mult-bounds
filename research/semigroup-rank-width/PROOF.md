# A multiplicative-rank refinement of chronological width

## Statement

Use exactly the fixed default-height rule, chronological batches and integer
width recurrence of codyw912's [PR #4638](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/4638),
`research/chronological-width-bound`, sections 1-4. In particular the target
ranks have positive integer multiplicities W_k, paid ranks have positive
integer multiplicities P_j, and

```
2 <= k_min <= k <= m = max(target ranks),
1 <= j <= j_max < m,
W = sum W_k, P = sum P_j.
```

Base widths below m are one. At rank n >= m the cap is floor(n/m) and
the height is floor(log_(k_min)(floor(n/m))). Let A be the set of all target
and paid ranks. Rank one is permitted and contributes a zero valuation vector.
Let Pi be the prime support of A and d its size. Form the integer matrix

```
V = (v_p(a)) with rows p in Pi and columns a in A,
r = rank_Q(V).
```

Then 1 <= r <= d, and the same width sequence satisfies

\[
\log B_N=O((\log N)^{r+1}).
\]

The constant depends on the fixed rule. This replaces d+1 in #4638 by r+1.
The degree strictly decreases when the valuation vectors span a proper
subspace of Q^d. It is an upper bound and does not assert a matching lower
bound for the actual widths.

## The new counting lemma

Let Gamma be the multiplicative semigroup generated by A, including one.
Choose a set I of r prime coordinates whose r rows of V are independent.
Such rows exist by the definition of rank. Projection to those coordinates
is injective on the column space U of V. Indeed its restriction has rank r,
equal to dim U, so its kernel is zero.

For x in Gamma, the complete valuation vector v(x) belongs to U. If two
products x,y have the same valuations at the primes in I, injectivity gives
v(x)=v(y) at every prime in Pi. Unique prime factorization gives x=y.
This argument does not require a unique expression as a product of generators.

For 1 <= x <= X, each selected coordinate is an integer in
[0,floor(log_p X)]. Therefore

\[
|\Gamma\cap[1,X]|
\le\prod_{p\in I}(1+\lfloor\log_p X\rfloor)
\le(1+\lfloor\log_2 X\rfloor)^r.                 \tag{1}
\]

The selected-prime box can contain vectors outside the projected semigroup.
Surjectivity is unnecessary. Selecting arbitrary r primes is insufficient;
their rows must be independent on U. For example projecting the semigroup
generated by 6 and 10 only to the 3-adic coordinate identifies 1 and 10.

## Application to the inherited width divisor

The following facts are cited from #4638, sections 2-4, rather than
re-established by a source replay here. Source counts S(gamma) are constant
on Gamma plateaus. At cap C their bound is

\[
1\le S(\gamma)\le W(W+P)^G,
\qquad G=\lfloor\log_{k_{\min}} C\rfloor.
\]

Every recursive child has rank at most alpha=(m-1)/m times its parent rank.
With tau=m ceil(log_2 k_min), every descending rank chain visits each plateau
at most tau times. For every N >= m, this yields the all-prime divisor

\[
B_n\mid Q_N=
\prod_{\substack{\gamma\in\Gamma\\\gamma\le C}} S(\gamma)^\tau,
\qquad C=\lfloor N/m\rfloor,\quad 0\le n\le N.       \tag{2}
\]

The prime factors of S(gamma) need not belong to Pi. Equation (2) retains
all of them. The new projection is used only to count plateau locations;
it does not discard any source-count or width prime.

Combining (1) and (2) gives the explicit new estimate

\[
\log_2 B_n\le
\tau\prod_{p\in I}(1+\lfloor\log_p C\rfloor)
\bigl(\log_2 W+G\log_2(W+P)\bigr),\quad n\le N.     \tag{3}
\]

There are O((log N)^r) box points, and the final factor is O(log N).
This proves the stated all-rank bound. Replacing the two logarithms in the
last factor by their integer ceilings gives a computable integer exponent
b_N with B_n <= 2^b_N. All width and chronological correctness premises
are unchanged. No assumption that gcd cancellation is small or absent is used.

## A strict degree improvement

Take target multiplicities W_6=W_36=1 and paid multiplicities P_1=P_6=1.
This is an admissible rank pattern for the integer recurrence, since 6<36.
No physical native rule realizing it is asserted. Its prime support is {2,3},
but its valuation columns are (1,1), (2,2) and (0,0), of rational rank one.
The old width theorem gives log B_N=O((log N)^3); (3) gives
log B_N=O((log N)^2) for this same recurrence.

The semigroup is exactly {6^j:j>=0}, so its exact plateau count is
1+floor(log_6 C). This example witnesses a strict decrease of the proved
upper-bound degree, not a measured asymptotic separation between two
width sequences. This contribution changes no sequence.

The first proved refinement is the finish line. Adaptive heights, optimal
coordinate choices and sharper actual-width growth are left open.
