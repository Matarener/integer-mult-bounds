## Complete ordinary time bound

I do not assign them an unmeasured absolute machine-time value.

    n^(1-c)w<=2^c e^(1-c+c^2)<(17/16)e^(1-a).

    T_B(e)<=4283584 C V e^(1-a)<4300000 C V e^(1-a). (2)

For e<2^20, elementary fallback satisfies
e^a<2^(20/512)<2, so its cost also fits (2). The retained selector's

The bit wrapper pads each ordinary field by a factor below q and

## Consequence and limit

The resulting ordinary saving exceeds PR2979's stated complex endpoint
C_complex=1729033982646513517717/10^24 by

    354445191478901059996907/(332929*10^24)>0.

Among reciprocal couplings 1/j, j>=2, only j=577 satisfies both
1/j<b_BIT and (j-1)/j^2>C_complex. I check 1/576>b_BIT>1/577,
the passing value at 577 and the failing value at 578; monotonicity
of (j-1)/j^2 for j>=2 completes that restricted statement. I claim
no optimum over arbitrary rational couplings or practical runtimes.

I close this ordinary-rate implication under the retained mathematical
premises. A complex consumer still needs its own stopping exponent,
root spacing/reservations, production binding and complete analytic/packing
transfer. I do not substitute this rate for those obligations or adopt
PR2979's conditional multiplication claim as the selected frontier.
