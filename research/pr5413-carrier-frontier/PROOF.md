## 1. Complete paid charge

The source has 10,928 productive fields, 64,260 paid jobs and ambient rank 145.
For raw moments T_t and P_t, tested saving s, t=1-s, u>=s and integer v>=1, put

```
alpha=P_t/T_t,
gamma=P_(t+u)/(c^u*T_t),
delta=10928*b/(c^s*T_t),  1<c<b<145.
```

The higher-moment theorem in `research/higher-moment-strictification/README.md`
applies to the chronological v-fold product, completion to at least b^v on each
actual productive owner, and splitting at c^v. Its complete normalized upper
bound is `alpha^v+2*gamma^v+2*delta^v`. For R>J, the final incomplete split block
is included in `q*J^t+r^t<=2*R*J^(-s)`; the higher moment is valid because
`t+u>=1`. Completion includes the same final-block allowance and every owner.
The final productive moment is at least the raw T_t^v, so normalization by that
smaller quantity is conservative. No new field or unpaid child is introduced.

The old `.97` bounds and `4/2^32` debit were sufficient upper estimates of those
same charged jobs. The new certificate evaluates all three terms directly with
outward rational bounds. It does not add the old sufficient debit as a second
copy of physical work. In particular the fixed-kappa point has gamma>.97 but
its full paid bound is still strictly below one. The source's old conjunction
correctly rejects that point; its changed application is explicit here.

The public `exact_moments.py` helper is authenticated and reused with attribution,
not called an independent implementation. Its rational logarithm/exponential
enclosures are rounded outward before integer powers; each positive paid term
is rounded upward again, and their exact sum must be below one.

## 3. A rational-gap carrier grid

Set `H_j=ceil(j/d)` for positive integers j. A successful grid point obeys
`H_j>8v` and `(H_j+1)^p<2^j<=2^(d*H_j)`. Thus at N=2^H_j the leaf bound is
strictly below `N^(1-s+d)=N^(1-uniform)`. The new construction does not require
the numerator of d to be one; every rank and program count remains integral.

For the implemented domain `0<d<1/16`, the binary search finds the first j:

- If `j<=4p`, then `H_j>=1/d>16`, hence `(H_j+1)^p>2^(4p)>=2^j`.
- For `j>=4p`, `H_(j+1)-H_j<=ceil(1/d)`. Therefore
  `(H_(j+1)+1)/(H_j+1)<=1+(1+d)/j`. Raising this ratio to p gives less than
  `exp(17/64)<2`, using `log 2>1/2`. The left comparison grows by less than
  two while the right comparison doubles. Once the grid succeeds it stays
  successful, and the preceding tested grid point fails exactly.

No minimal integer H, global compiler optimum or maximal kappa is claimed.
