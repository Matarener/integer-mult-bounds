## 1. The exact current offer

Let V be the complex address-vector space at any positive lifted digit width. There are 112 fields in each main bank x,y and eight mode fields h. The other 400 helper fields have autonomous Gaussian endpoints and are unchanged throughout the construction below. Let D be the Gaussian on the eight noncentral address axes, Q the central-axis Gaussian, and R=DQ=QD the full rank-nine Gaussian. These are applied diagonally to their respective banks.

The actual coupled provider M has the endpoint

    X = D x − R y + R W h,
    Y = D y,
    H = D(h − Z y).                                  (1)

It has 632 arbitrary original inputs, nine arbitrary owned inputs cleared before use and zero on return, and paid Gaussian profile

    P = {1:2193, 2:412, 3:510, 4:127, 5:40}.

Its autonomous helper profile is

    H_aut = {4:28, 7:7, 8:47, 9:318}.

The eight fields H in (1) are coupled and do not add eight independent Gaussian targets.

The source supplies ordinary finite maps

    W = diag(K_p^(-1)) M_0 diag(K_m)/2,
    Z = diag(K_m^(-1)) M_0^T diag(K_p)/2,

where M_0 is a real scalar 112 by 8 matrix with M_0^T M_0=56I, and all K_p,K_m are actual real orthogonal monomial address maps. Its exact identities are

    ZW = 14I,       WZ = I−C,
    R W = W R,     Z R = R Z.                        (2)

The R on each side acts on the appropriate main or mode bank. All scalar coefficients are applied once, outside the address tensor power. Neither W nor Z is assumed to intertwine D or Q separately. That distinction is essential.

The ordinary maps in this note mean their explicit scalar/monomial implementations. They have real nonrecursive costs. They are not Gaussian oracle calls or arbitrary dense changes of address basis.

## 2. A selected mode projection

Fix a coordinate subset J of {1,...,8}, let E select those coordinates, let k=|J|, and put F=I−E. Thus E²=E, F²=F, EF=FE=0. These are scalar mode-bank maps and commute with D,Q,R on the mode fields.

Define three explicit operations on the original input/output banks:

    P_E: h ← h + E Z y,
    Q_E: apply Q on H_j for each j in J,
    O_E: X ← X − W E H.

Each P_E and O_E is an ordinary invertible shear: its inverse changes the addition sign. Q_E consists of k charged rank-one jobs. No original input is initialized, identified with another input, or thrown away.

## Theorem 1. An all-input partially autonomous offer

Execute P_E, the unchanged coupled provider M, Q_E, then O_E. The complete endpoint is

    Y = D y,
    E H = R E h,
    F H = D(F h − F Z y),
    X = D x − R C_E y + R W F h,                    (3)

where

    C_E = I − W E Z.                                (4)

All 632 original inputs are arbitrary. Exactly the selected k mode helpers are autonomous full rank-nine jobs.

**Proof.** After P_E the mode input is h+EZy. Substitute this into (1). The mode output before Q_E is

    D(h+EZy−Zy)=D(h−FZy).

Applying E gives DEh; applying the k central Q jobs therefore returns REh. The complementary mode output is D(Fh−FZy) and is unchanged by Q_E.

The main output before O_E is

    Dx−Ry+RW(h+EZy)
      = Dx−R(I−WEZ)y+RWh.

The selected mode output entering O_E is REh. The intertwiner RW=WR in (2) gives W(REh)=RWEh, so subtracting WEH removes exactly that part. The remaining main term is RWFh. This proves every identity in (3), including its operator order. The selected output REh depends only on its original selected input, not on x,y or any unselected helper. ∎

The construction uses no interchange of D with W or Z. In particular, substituting an assumed D-intertwiner here would change the source contract.

## Theorem 2. Exact paid and autonomous inventories

The construction of Theorem 1 has paid profile

and autonomous helper profile

    H_E = {4:28, 7:7, 8:47, 9:318+k}.                (5)

It uses the source's nine owned fields, with additional ordinary temporary fields if the chosen explicit W/Z implementation needs them; all such extra fields can be zero on return. The source's other 400 helpers retain their original all-input endpoints and are never used as zero scratch.

**Proof.** The only newly inserted nonordinary operations are exactly k Q calls. P_E reads y, adds its prescribed E Z image to the selected original h fields, and changes no helper outside that selected bank. It is an invertible input shear, not preparation of a supposedly dependent original input. O_E is another invertible shear. Source ownership and endpoint guarantees apply on all such inputs. Theorem 1 supplies k new autonomous R endpoints and leaves the other 400 autonomous source endpoints untouched. This proves (5). ∎

The positive helper-only moment change is k(9^t−1) at exponent t. This change is real as a statement about the autonomous helper inventory, but its changed main and remaining mode operators must also be consumed. It is not a complete target-minus-paid supplier moment by itself.

## Theorem 3. The selected code is invertible, bounded and full-R compatible

Let A_E=WEZ. Then

    A_E²=14 A_E,
    C_E^(-1)=I−A_E/13.                              (6)

For coordinate E, C_E is self-adjoint, equals −13 on the image of WE, and equals 1 on the kernel of EZ. It commutes with full R. Its spectral norm is at most 13 and its inverse norm is at most 1.

**Proof.** Using ZW=14I and E²=E gives A_E²=WE(ZW)EZ=14WEZ. Multiplying I−A_E by I−A_E/13 gives I+A_E(−1−1/13+14/13)=I, proving the inverse formula on both sides. The actual real orthogonal monomial normalizers imply Z=W*, because the scalar matrix M_0 is real. Hence A_E=W E W* is self-adjoint. By ZW=14I, the map W/√14 is an isometry from the mode space into the main-bank space. Thus A_E/14 is the orthogonal projection onto the image of WE. The claimed eigenspaces and norm bounds follow. Finally, (2) and ER=RE give RWEZ=WEZR, so C_E commutes with R. ∎

The coefficient 1/13 in (6) is an explicit rational mathematical formula. It is not treated as a free dyadic primitive. The routines P_E,O_E themselves use the source's dyadic scalar/monomial coefficients and require no evaluation of C_E^(-1).

## Theorem 4. The complete mixed endpoint is invertible

The endpoint (3), including all eight mode outputs, is an isomorphism of all original inputs. A literal inverse is obtained in the following order:

    y = D^(-1) Y,
    E h = R^(-1) E H,
    F h = D^(-1) F H + F Z y,
    x = D^(-1)[X + R C_E y − R W F h].             (7)

The other 400 helper jobs are inverted by their own original inverse Gaussian maps.

**Proof.** The first three equations invert the corresponding triangular equations in (3). Their right sides are known by that point, and the invertible factors D,R exist. The last equation then inverts the main X equation. Direct substitution reconstructs every original field. This also follows from T_E=O_E Q_E M P_E as a product of invertible maps on the persistent inputs. ∎

Formula (7) is a correctness identity; it is not the most economical way to implement the inverse. The same-price construction below uses the already supplied inverse of M rather than separately charging every occurrence in (7).

## Theorem 6. Linear transposition supplies the full reciprocal dual

A fixed finite linear algorithm on role fields, built from scalar ADD/SCALE, monomial address maps and Gaussian children, has an exact transpose algorithm. It uses one transposed Gaussian child for each original child, plus finite ordinary work and owned storage. In the actual Gaussian alphabet, transposing a rank-r child preserves its rank and gives an explicitly monomial-normalized rank-r child. Applying this to the inverse construction in Theorem 5 gives a full T_E^(-T) offer with profile P_E.

**Proof.** Introduce one adjoint buffer for each version of each forward wire, all initially zero. Seed the specified output buffers with the arbitrary dual input fields. Process operations in reverse order. For z=a u+b v, add a z* to u* and b z* to v*. A monomial map U passes its adjoint by U^T, another monomial map including the exact transposed phase. A Gaussian child A passes its adjoint by A^T once, after all its downstream adjoint contributions have been accumulated. Thus shared forward wires do not duplicate a Gaussian call. COPY is handled by accumulation into its source adjoint, and an unused cleared wire has zero adjoint injection.

The invariant is the formal bilinear pairing between wire values and wire adjoints. Every reverse replacement preserves that pairing and removes one forward instruction. At the original input wires it proves the exact transpose action. No original runtime data are needed in this reverse construction because all coefficients are fixed and the algorithm is linear. Owned buffers are explicitly cleared after their final reads; arbitrary initial owned dirt is first cleared.

Every transpose in this construction is taken on the actual physical linear map. A bookkeeping zero retag is physical identity and remains identity; it is not replaced by an unproved nonzero frame conversion. For a physical Gaussian child, use its actual factorization U=M_L C_r M_R, with ordinary monomials and the canonical symmetric tensor Gaussian C_r. Then U^T=M_R^T C_r M_L^T has the same one rank-r request and explicit ordinary wrappers. Exact phase is retained because the operator factors themselves are transposed. The finite source inverse algorithm in Theorem 5 has precisely the original Gaussian ranks. Its transpose therefore gives T_E^(-T) with those same ranks and the same k additional central jobs. ∎

This is a mathematical finite-template construction with a larger but finite ordinary/owned allowance. It is not a claim that the old in-place transcript is simply reversed without treating COPY and CLEAR correctly. The dual's complete mixed endpoint is the actual inverse transpose, not a transpose relabeled by commuting an even D² through C_E.

## Theorem 7. All positive digit widths and finite guards

Theorems 1–6 hold at every positive lifted digit width under the source's exact physical-map hypotheses. The added ordinary preparation, decoding and transposition work has finite-template prefix and suffix norm bounds independent of that digit width, with the usual explicit coordinate-to-field rounding conversion. All additional nonrecursive work and owned storage have fixed finite bounds before the external input length grows.

**Proof.** The source identities (1)–(2) hold under the exact native lifted operator assignment. E is a scalar role projection; it is not tensor-powered on address columns. P_E and O_E use the fixed source monomial/owner words, so they lift with exactly the original scalar coefficients. Q_E is an actual rank-one Gaussian child at that lifted width. Every algebraic composition above therefore remains valid.

In the maximum-over-fields Euclidean norm, each monomial/child is unitary. Each scalar ADD by c has norm at most 1+|c|, and every fixed scalar program has the product of its finitely many such factors as a prefix/suffix bound. The source provider already supplies a finite such envelope. Multiplying by the extra finite P_E/O_E envelopes and the finite transpose-program envelope gives another constant depending on the template, not on digit width. Local rounding errors propagate by the sum of their norms times the appropriate suffix bound. The count of coordinate roundings and the dimension conversion must be included in any native implementation's actual precision budget; no dimension-free coordinate claim is inferred merely from a field norm. ∎

## 3. Honest endpoint cases and the completion question

For k=8, F=0, so all 408 helpers are autonomous, the main block is

    X=Dx−RC y,       Y=Dy.

This is the already documented center-free coded offer. Its profile P+8H1 equals `{1:2201,2:412,3:510,4:127,5:40}`. Recovering that endpoint by a shorter block proof is not a new profile improvement.

For k=0 all eight original modes remain coupled. If one instead redesignates all eight as owned zero inputs, (1) gives the ordinary uncoded main block and 400 independent helpers, with the unselected outputs subsequently cleared only because those fields are owned. This is the already documented relation-restricted offer. Intermediate k can similarly produce an independent-input smaller offer with a rank-k code, but its reciprocal dual must be rebuilt after that restriction: principal restriction and inverse transpose do not commute in general.

The full 632-input family in Theorems 1–7 avoids that restriction and has an actual same-profile inverse and reciprocal dual. Its selected helpers are genuine autonomous outputs; its remaining modes are honestly coupled. A larger complete κ would require a caller that consumes this exact mixed interface, or a cheaper standardization of the rank-k code together with all remaining mode effects. No such stronger complete caller is asserted here.

This theorem refers to the physically standardized maps of Sections 1-2, where W and Z are actual ordinary monomial/scalar programs and C = I-WZ intertwines the full R. It is not inferred from a logical-coordinate code alone.

**Proof.** Use the raw physical endpoint directly. First apply Q to all 112 Y fields, obtaining Y_1 = Ry. Next apply Q to the eight coupled H fields, obtaining H_1 = Rh-RZy; add ZY_1 to obtain H_2 = Rh, using the actual intertwiner RZ = ZR. Ordinary decoding can use fixed temporary buffers which are cleared before use and after their last read. Thus arbitrary dirty owned inputs do not contaminate the outputs, and all owned buffers return to zero.

The full linear action is diag(R,R) on the two main banks and the inherited autonomous helper action on all 408 helpers. Consequently all 632 original directions are independent and preserved. The complete productive profile is {4:28,7:7,8:47,9:550}. This complete profile must be compared directly with its paid profile in the actual supplier inequality. ## Theorem 9. Central promotion of mixed triangular interfaces

Let a finite ordered bank have arbitrary independent inputs u_1,...,u_m and exact outputs

    v_i = D_i u_i + sum_{j<i}(D_i A_ij + R_i B_ij)u_j.

Suppose Q_i D_i = R_i, and the explicitly ordinary inter-bank maps satisfy A_ij R_j = R_i A_ij and B_ij R_j = R_i B_ij. No D_i/A_ij or Q_i/A_ij commutation is required. Then the interface can be converted to all independent R_i u_i outputs with precisely one additional Q_i request per bank entry, plus finite ordinary work and owned storage. Any untouched autonomous blocks retain their original outputs.

**Proof.** Process i in increasing order, storing the already obtained r_j = R_j u_j. Subtract sum_{j<i} B_ij r_j from v_i. By the B intertwining identity this cancels precisely the R_i B_ij u_j terms. Apply Q_i once. The resulting value is r_i + sum_{j<i} R_i A_ij u_j. Subtract sum_{j<i} A_ij r_j. By the A intertwining identity the result is exactly r_i. Induction proves the claim for all independent inputs. Every operation acts on the original physical maps; all scalar and monomial coefficients are fixed. Compute any required ordinary temporary from initialized zero storage, clear it after its final read, and retain every original productive output. This gives explicit finite cleanup even for arbitrary initial owned dirt. A finite prefix/suffix envelope follows from the products of elementary operation norms as in Theorem 7. The actual inverse and transpose constructions give reciprocal exact algorithms with the same Gaussian ranks, with ordinary work and storage allowed to increase by fixed finite amounts.

For the actual provider, order the affected banks as (y,h,x). Its nonzero coefficient maps are A_hy = -Z, B_xy = -I and B_xh = W. Thus Theorem 8 is an instance on 112+8+112 fields. The mixed triangular lemma explains why the central promotion is valid even though the actual main code fails to commute with the partial Gaussian D: no such commutation appears in its induction.
