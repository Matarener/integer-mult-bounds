# Recognizing Frobenius-truncated algebras from a multiplication table

This note gives a recognition criterion and an explicit algebra isomorphism
for a supplied multiplication table over a prime field. It answers a restricted
part of [Ali Romman's PR5459 handoff](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5459):
the inputs and output already refer to one common vector space. It does not
recognize an arbitrary three-factor tensor up to independent basis changes,
extract its nucleus, or supply the reductions needed to obtain that table.
It does not change the selected multiplication bound.

## 1. Input contract and criterion

Let `p` be a prime and let `A` be a nonzero, `n`-dimensional vector space over
`F_p`, supplied by its structure constants. First check commutativity and
associativity on a basis. Recover the identity by solving the `n^2` linear
coordinate equations `u*b_j = b_j` for its `n` unknown coordinates, and check
the resulting element on both sides of every basis vector. Reject if the
system is inconsistent or any check fails. Commutativity makes a left unit
two-sided, and a two-sided unit is unique. These finite checks establish the
algebra laws for all inputs by bilinearity and trilinearity. A malformed table
is rejected before the criterion below.
Primality of `p` is a separate input condition: arithmetic modulo a composite
integer is not a substitute for `F_p`.

Write `F(a) = a^p`. This is an `F_p`-linear endomorphism because `A` is
commutative of characteristic `p`. Let `E` be the least nonnegative integer
such that `p^E >= n`, and set

```
J = ker(F^E),             Q = J / J^2,
K_e = ker(F^e),           0 <= e <= E,
V_e = (K_e + J^2) / J^2,  0 <= e <= E.
```

Here `F^0` is the identity, so `K_0 = 0`; `J^2` denotes the span of products
of pairs of elements of `J`. Every `K_e` lies in `J`, so `V_e` is a subspace
of `Q`. The spaces `V_e` form an increasing flag from `0` to `Q`.
Define

```
d_e = dim(V_e) - dim(V_(e-1)),       1 <= e <= E,
N = p^(sum_(e=1)^E e*d_e).
```

**Theorem.** The checked table is isomorphic, as a unital `F_p`-algebra, to

```
B = F_p[x_1,...,x_r] / (x_i^(p^h_i) : 1 <= i <= r),
```

with positive heights `h_i`, if and only if

1. `J` has codimension one in `A`; and
2. `N = n`.

The multiset of heights is intrinsic: height `e` occurs `d_e` times. An empty
list is allowed, in which case `B = F_p` and `n = 1`. Acceptance produces
generators `g_i` of the prescribed heights and the invertible matrix whose
columns are their ordered monomials. This matrix, together with its inverse,
is an explicit transporter between the common-basis multiplication tables.

The word "transporter" here means an algebra isomorphism with the same map
on both inputs and the output. It is not a general tensor-orbit transporter.

## 2. The Frobenius kernel recovers the nilradical

Let `R` be the set of nilpotent elements of `A`. In a commutative algebra,
`R` is an ideal: products with arbitrary elements remain nilpotent, and a
sufficiently large characteristic-power Frobenius kills a sum of nilpotents.
Choose a vector-space basis `b_1,...,b_s` of `R` and positive integers `t_i`
with `b_i^t_i = 0`. The ideal generated by these basis elements is `R`.
Every product of more than `sum_i(t_i-1)` generators contains a factor
`b_i^t_i`, so `R` is nilpotent.

The chain `R, R^2, ...` decreases strictly until it reaches zero. Indeed, if
`R^k = R^(k+1)` is nonzero, multiplication by further powers of `R` makes all
later powers equal, contrary to nilpotence. Since `1` is not nilpotent,
`dim R <= n-1`, and therefore `R^n = 0`.

Every `a` in `R` consequently satisfies `a^(p^E) = 0`, since `p^E >= n`.
Conversely, every element killed by this power is nilpotent. Thus

```
J = ker(F^E) = R,    J^n = 0.
```

This derivation does not assume that `A` is local. If `J` has codimension
one, the scalar copy of `F_p` maps isomorphically onto `A/J`. Every element
outside `J` then has the form `c*1 + j`, where `c != 0` and `j` is nilpotent,
and is a unit by a finite geometric series. Thus `J` is the unique maximal
ideal, and `A` is local with residue field exactly `F_p`.

The codimension test matters. An extension field such as `F_4` viewed as a
two-dimensional `F_2`-algebra has zero nilradical but is outside this family;
its radical has codimension two. Products of fields are also excluded.

## 3. Necessity and recovery of the heights

Suppose `A` is the displayed algebra `B`. Its monomials

```
x_1^a_1 ... x_r^a_r,   0 <= a_i < p^h_i,
```

form a basis, its radical is `(x_1,...,x_r)`, and its tangent quotient
`J/J^2` has basis the classes of `x_1,...,x_r`. In particular, `J` has
codimension one and `n = p^(sum_i h_i)`, so every `h_i <= E`.

If `h_i <= e`, the element `x_i` is killed by `F^e`; hence its tangent class
belongs to `V_e`. For the converse, expand an element of `J` into its linear
part and terms of total degree at least two. Frobenius raises every monomial
separately to its `p^e` power. If the linear coefficient of `x_i` is nonzero
and `h_i > e`, its power contributes a nonzero coefficient to
`x_i^(p^e)`. No other linear term contributes that monomial. No term of
degree at least two contributes it either: its surviving power has total
degree at least `2*p^e`; terms killed by a truncation contribute nothing.
Thus this coefficient cannot cancel.

It follows that

```
V_e = span{x_i + J^2 : h_i <= e}.
```

Therefore `d_e` counts exactly the variables of height `e`, and the dimension
test `N = n` is necessary. The proof is invariant under an arbitrary common
change of basis in `A`; the identity and radical need not be coordinate axes
in the supplied table.

## 4. Sufficiency and an explicit transporter

Assume the two tests pass. For `e = 1,...,E`, extend a basis of `V_(e-1)`
to a basis of `V_e`, and choose the new representatives directly in `K_e`.
Linear elimination on a basis of `K_e` modulo `J^2` constructs such lifts.
Call the resulting elements `g_1,...,g_r` and assign height `h_i = e` to
each lift added at step `e`. Their classes form a basis of `J/J^2`, and

```
g_i^(p^h_i) = 0.
```

The classes are chosen by first entry into the flag. A lift of height `e`
cannot already lie in `K_(e-1)`, since its class would then belong to
`V_(e-1)`. No minimal-exponent test beyond the flag is needed.

The substitution `x_i -> g_i` defines a unital homomorphism `phi: B -> A`,
because all defining power relations hold. It is surjective. One way to see
this is the finite nilpotent form of Nakayama's lemma. Let `C` be the
`F_p`-subalgebra generated by `1,g_1,...,g_r`. Tangent spanning gives
`J = span{g_i} + J^2`. For each `k >= 1`, express every factor of a product
in `J^k` by this formula. The product of its linear parts lies in `C`, and
every other term lies in `J^(k+1)`. Hence

```
J^k is contained in C + J^(k+1).
```

Starting from `A = F_p*1 + J` and repeating this containment through
`J^n = 0` gives `A = C`. This is also a direct proof of the particular
Nakayama implication used here.

The monomial basis of `B` has size `N`, and the dimension test gives
`dim B = N = n = dim A`. A surjective linear map between these equal finite
dimensions is invertible. Therefore `phi` is the required isomorphism.

For a concrete certificate, order the exponent tuples in a declared fixed
order and form the matrix

```
P[:,a] = g_1^a_1 ... g_r^a_r
```

in the supplied basis of `A`. Its constant column is the recovered unit.
The certificate consists of the heights, the generators, `P` and `P^-1`.
An independent checker can verify invertibility, the unit column, every
defining generator power and every basis-product identity

```
mu_A(P*u_a, P*u_b) = P*mu_B(u_a,u_b).
```

These `n^2` identities imply the identity for all pairs by bilinearity.
They check the actual transporter rather than trusting the recognition
path or the inferred heights. Equivalently,
`mu_B(u,v) = P^-1*mu_A(P*u,P*v)`.

## 5. Why tangent Frobenius alone is insufficient

The filtration uses images of kernels in the algebra before quotienting:
`V_e = (ker(F^e) + J^2)/J^2`. It must not be replaced by the kernel of the
induced Frobenius on `J/J^2`. Indeed, `a^p` lies in `J^p`, which is contained
in `J^2` for every `p >= 2`, so the latter induced map is always zero.

For the explicit example `A = F_2[t]/(t^4)`, the radical is
`span{t,t^2,t^3}`, with square `span{t^2,t^3}`. Squaring gives

```
(a*t + b*t^2 + c*t^3)^2 = a*t^2.
```

Thus `ker F = span{t^2,t^3}`, `V_1 = 0`, and `V_2 = J/J^2`.
The correct height list is `(2)`, and the predicted dimension is four.
Frobenius on the tangent quotient alone would give height `(1)` and the
wrong predicted dimension two.

The dimension test also cannot be dropped. For the square-zero algebra
`F_2 + J` with `dim J = 3` and `J^2 = 0`, all three tangent generators have
height one, but `dim A = 4` while the predicted truncated-polynomial
dimension is `2^3 = 8`. The generators give a surjection with additional
relations, not an isomorphism to the proposed model.

## 6. Finite algorithm, cost and the one-dimensional edge

All steps are finite linear algebra and arithmetic on the supplied table:

1. Validate commutativity and associativity on basis vectors, recover the
   unit by solving its linear equations, and check it on both sides.
2. Compute Frobenius on basis vectors by repeated squaring, its successive
   kernels through `E`, and a basis of `J^2` from pairwise radical products.
3. Compute the flag dimensions and adapted lifts by Gaussian elimination;
   reject unless the codimension and dimension tests pass.
4. Enumerate the `n` model monomials after acceptance, compute their images,
   invert the resulting matrix, and check every basis-product identity.

For fixed prime `p`, this is a deterministic polynomial algorithm in `n`.
There are at most `ceil(log_2 n)` Frobenius levels. Dense table multiplication
uses at most `O(n^3)` field operations, and the table-law and final
basis-product checks use polynomially many such operations (a conservative
bound for a direct implementation is `O(n^6 + n^4 log p)`). No search over
all algebra elements, all bases or all automorphisms is required. Computing
the exact candidate dimension is polynomial: its bit length is at most
`O(n*(log n + log p))`, and is `O(n log n)` for fixed `p`. The implementation
may compute that exact integer. Optionally, capped multiplication can stop
as soon as the candidate exceeds `n`; it is not needed for the complexity
guarantee.

Conditional on a prime input, ordinary field arithmetic and powering are
also polynomial in `log p`; primality certification must be accounted for
separately in any variable-characteristic bit-complexity claim. In particular,
trial division is sufficient for bounded prime fixtures but does not by itself
establish a polynomial primality algorithm in the bit length of `p`.

When `n = 1`, `E = 0`, `J = ker F^0 = 0`, the flag and height list are
empty, and the empty product gives `N = 1`. The recovered unit is nonzero and
forms a basis, so `P` is its single coordinate column. This covers `F_p`
without introducing a height-zero variable. The zero-dimensional zero ring
is outside the input contract.

## 7. Scope, credit and remaining work

The criterion establishes the promised algebra class from a checked common-
basis multiplication table over `F_p`, and constructs an isomorphism to its
standard monomial model. The written theorem covers every such dimension;
the package's finite fixtures corroborate it and are not a proof-kernel check.

The immediate source is Ali Romman's
[PR5459 at `bef3575ddb19c44434a7e2460a353eac7c4d9387`](https://github.com/aliromman/integer-mult-bounds/blob/bef3575ddb19c44434a7e2460a353eac7c4d9387/research/frobenius-tensor-characters/README.md),
which supplies the unequal-height family, height flag and explicit
recognition/transporter handoff. His [PR5316](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5316)
supplies the truncated-polynomial predecessor. Nick Petalas's
[PR5252](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5252) supplies
the finite-etale comparison, nilpotent follow-up and tensor normalization
context; David Ganz and the retained atlas lineage retain their credit.
Nilradicals, tangent generators, [Nakayama's lemma](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00DV)
and truncated-polynomial presentations are classical commutative algebra.
Section 4 gives the finite nilpotent argument used here directly. No general literature
priority is claimed for this criterion.

Open next questions are the reduction from an independently based input
tensor to this checked table, with certified factor maps, and an extension
to supplied non-prime finite fields with the appropriate semilinear
Frobenius calculations. Neither follows merely by applying the present
`F_p`-linear kernel routine. Determinant-character extraction can subsequently
use the verified heights under its separately pinned theorem. Physical
coefficient preparation, native realization, precision, movement, supplier
admission and a new multiplication exponent remain outside this result.
