## Whole-row split and actual IDLE child reassociation

Let a current row range have size R, with `R=L*U`. Splitting an entire row by
`r=L*g+w` gives a bijection

```
{0,...,R-1} × Omega  <->  {0,...,L-1} × {0,...,U-1} × Omega,
```

where Omega is the entire within-row rectangle, including both front fields,
active slots, back field, spectators and record suffix. No compact or active
bit participates in the row quotient. Consequently each role receives all of
Omega; address completeness is independent of the payload's dirty values.
Repeated legal divisions use mixed-radix row digits, and the inverse formula
restores every original row. A parent/child packet may be correlated with other
owners; this does not change its complete address set.

For each actual IDLE permutation p, the child prefix labels are
`(p[a],j)` for `a<r`, and the complement labels are `(p[a],j)` for `r<=a<145`.
The two lists partition the complete145f labels. Interpreting the second list
as right spectators is a Cartesian reassociation, not a projection or a
reallocation of independent coordinates. The parent controls remain complete
because the primitive restored them before child entry. An assumed child that
preserves spectators and returns its full packet therefore permits the inverse
route on return. The checker verifies all15 actual lists at f=1,2,7, including
complete complementary labels; a dropped spectator is rejected.

If one pads a row count R once to a multiple of a stock product P with R>=P,
then `R'=ceil(R/P)P<2R`. Added rows contain the whole Omega and zero payload;
no partial front/back field is padded. They can be removed only after the
assumed complete linear invocation returns each zero row to zero. Our checker
tests mixed-radix boundary values, including huge integers, but neither assigns
final P nor proves the mathematical endpoint that makes padded rows deletable.
The generic bijection is predecessor work; the contribution is its explicit
join to these source routes and their actual child complements.
