# Remaining-tail pair-support envelope

Prepared for SHIN09-git with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance. Apache-2.0.
This is a scoped elementary lemma and finite corroboration, not proof-kernel or
independent human acceptance.

## Contract

Let q_0=0,q_1,...,q_k be a simple path in the n-dimensional binary cube:
each edge flips one coordinate and all displayed vertices are distinct.
Seek a simple closed walk of total length L extending this prefix, with q_L=0
and all L nonclosing vertices distinct. Put r=L-k and m=r-1. A universal
completion requires every vector to be a pairwise XOR of its nonclosing vertices.
The closing zero is already present; it is not an additional new point.

Write d for Hamming distance, P={q_0,...,q_k}, and

    U = {x not in P : d(q_k,x) + d(x,0) <= r}.

For distinct x,y in U, put

    T(x,y) = d(x,y) + min(d(q_k,x)+d(y,0), d(q_k,y)+d(x,0)).

Define the nonzero difference envelope

    E2 = D(P)
         union {p XOR x : p in P, x in U}
         union {x XOR y : distinct x,y in U, T(x,y) <= r},

where D(P) is the set of differences of distinct points. The one-vertex
relaxation E1=D(P union U) ignores the shared tail budget of two future points.

**Necessary completion condition:** |U| >= m and E2 contains every nonzero
vector. Therefore a strict failure of either condition safely rejects the prefix.
Passing is inconclusive. E2 is a subset of E1.

## Proof

Every new nonclosing vertex x lies after q_k and before the final zero. The
numbers of edges on those two subpaths sum to r and dominate their Hamming
distances. Thus x belongs to U. Simplicity requires m distinct new vertices
outside P, proving the cardinality condition.

Consider any difference in an actual completion. Two old endpoints contribute
to D(P). An old and new endpoint contribute to the second set. For two new
endpoints, one occurs first on the suffix. If x precedes y, the three consecutive
subpaths q_k to x, x to y, and y to zero have total length r. Their distances sum
to at most r, so T(x,y)<=r. The opposite order is the second term of the minimum.
Thus every actual nonzero difference is in E2. Universality requires all of them.

The argument uses only necessary distance inequalities. It does not assert that
the vertices or pair choices in the envelope can be realized together. It may
allow repeated vertices, nonchronological choices and mutually incompatible
tails in its relaxation. Those permissions enlarge the envelope and cannot
produce a false rejection. Coordinate relabeling preserves all these conditions.

This proof does not use a functional partition, the cap 11, matching-edge
capacities, or any selected multiplication construction. The exposed verifier
enforces small bounded inputs; the finite evidence below is not a general
performance claim.

## Simplification and a strict three-edge witness

### Exact simplification: no future-pair loop is needed

In fact E2 equals D(P) union {p XOR x : p in P, x in U}.
For any compatible future pair, put z=x XOR y and t=q_k XOR z.
If x occurs first, the triangle inequality gives

    d(q_k,t)+d(t,0)
      = weight(z)+weight(q_k XOR x XOR y)
      <= d(x,y)+d(q_k,x)+d(y,0) <= r.

The reversed order gives the same conclusion. If t is already in P, z is
an old-old difference (t is not q_k because z is nonzero). Otherwise t belongs
to U, so z=q_k XOR t is an old-new difference. Thus every term added by the
joint future-pair loop is already present. Removing that loop is an exact
algebraic simplification, not an additional prune or weakened condition.

The final implementation uses this formula. A separate ordered-route checker
reconstructs the original E2 at all 21,640 prefixes and checks exact equality.
This simplification was found when a max-instead-of-min pair-route mutation
survived full envelope checks: any subset of the redundant pair terms gives
the same union. That mutation is correctly excluded from the final mutation
suite; the suite tests the actual simplified predicate's behavior instead.

### Literal witness

At the prefix word 010201320,

    P = (0,1,3,2,6,7,5,13,9,8), q_k=8, r=3,
    U = (4,10,12,16,24).

The one-vertex envelope covers all 31 nonzero masks. The only distinct future
pairs with T(x,y)<=3 are {4,12} and {16,24}. The two-vertex envelope misses
20 and 28. For example, the tempting pair {4,16} has T=5, so its difference
20 cannot be generated by two new points within the three remaining edges.

The only simple restored three-step suffixes are 232 and 434. Neither
completion is universal; the first uses four labels and the second five.
The verifier checks all 5^3 suffixes. Existing remaining-label, parity-union
and cap-11 tests pass this prefix.

## Auxiliary excess lower bound, with no incremental benefit in this domain

This separate trial DOES depend on the final class sizes supplied by the
five-label cap context: (6,6) at weight five; (5,7),(6,6),(7,5) at weight four.
It is not part of E2's proof or timed traversal.

For each functional class b, let a_b=|P_b|, d_b=|D(P_b)| and
h_b=|D(P_b union U_b)|. For a possible final size s_b, its support is at most

    v_b = min(h_b, d_b + C(s_b,2)-C(a_b,2)).

The second bound holds because each additional pair introduces at most one
new difference. Hence final separate-class excess is at least

    sum_b C(s_b,2) - sum_b v_b.

Minimize this lower bound over the listed size assignments with
a_b <= s_b <= a_b+|U_b|. No assignment means no completion in that stated
class-size contract. A minimum greater than 11 is a necessary rejection.

This trial rejects no additional prefix beyond the remaining-label, parity-union,
cap-11 and E1 controls on the complete 21,640-prefix domain. Its code and exact
negative classification remain reproducible; no engine integration is justified
by that trial.

## Comparison premises and enumeration completeness

The cheap comparison uses the credited PR3005 union-capacity inequalities.
At weight parity, a length-12 simple cycle has final class sizes (6,6).
Current sizes a,b and support union V require

    |V| + 30 - C(a,2)-C(b,2) >= 15,
    |cross_support| + 36 - a*b >= 16.

Restoration and use of every coordinate also require
popcount(q_k)+2*(5-used_labels)<=r. These are existing necessary conditions.

The cap-11 comparison is explicitly limited to five labels, length twelve
and weight-four/five functionals. It is credited to Ali Romman's PR4992
(exact head 12a0105150f5f44a19a2199db8506d1aa44b009a), following Mihir Srivastava
and Nick Petalas. Adding a point cannot lower pair excess: it adds a pairs and
at most a new differences. Therefore a complete-walk upper cap may reject a
prefix already exceeding it. The cap itself is not a new result here, and
our distance lemma does not inherit its proof obligations.

The DFS uses first-occurrence label order, simplicity and the forced final
singleton edge. Every labeled word has one representative under coordinate
permutation. No rotation/reversal quotient is used. The independent oracle
instead partitions the twelve positions into at most five nonempty even blocks,
ordered by their first positions. Restoration is equivalent to even block
sizes, so this enumerates every canonical restored word without a prefix
feasibility rule. Literal simple-state and XOR-coverage checks then select its
closed and universal sets. The entire sets, not just their counts, must match.
This representation is credited to Nick Petalas's PR3005; this implementation
does not import his code.

Every actual completion's future points and pair differences are checked against
E2 at every prefix. Additional raw-word controls scan 3^6 and 4^8 words without
canonical symmetry reduction. None of this runs or validates a seven-label
census, a maintained engine, a supplier, or the full multiplication theorem.


## Exact comparison boundary for the later matching-capacity result

PR5001 was completed during this session's initial snapshot and discovered at
the final targeted refresh. Its original full source and proof are retained
byte-for-byte. Only feasible_splits is invoked; this is not its registered replay.

compare_matching.py independently transcribes the stated finite predicate.
It enumerates the even final crossing count C, checks c<=C<=c+r+1 and at least
two crossings when both final classes are nonempty, and checks the required
stay counts s_j-C/2 against existing stays plus available matching edges.
Its edge-capacity calculation maximizes over how many one-new-vertex edges
are chosen, instead of copying the original greedy expression. All 108,195
predicate outputs agree exactly. The complete actual simple-cycle splits are
also checked for containment.

The 67 earlier strict examples all pass this matching predicate. No claim is
made that the predicates are incomparable in every dimension, or that their
combination improves runtime. The maintained-engine integration claimed in
Discussion4903 comment19139518 is another contributor's task.
