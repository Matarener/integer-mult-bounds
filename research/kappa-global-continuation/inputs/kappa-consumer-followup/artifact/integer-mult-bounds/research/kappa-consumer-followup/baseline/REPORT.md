patterns are infeasible on the fixed T360 clock. The complete reported

\[
\kappa=0.001730448360207635461740-10^{-46}-10^{-70}.

physical owner allocation. Separate finite native and changed-contract checks
are described below. The higher user value remains the research target to beat.

## The bit side leaves headroom under the same outer convention

Modern outer composition approaches the complex/bit primitive saving directly.
Applying the older `a/(1+a)` conversion to only one side gives a false bottleneck.
The byte-matching outer source states `saving = eps*q`, with all strict gaps
arbitrarily smaller than the endpoint grid. At 24 endpoint digits its fixed-grid
output is `C - 10^-46 - 10^-70`.

PR3700's retained source explicitly withdraws a previous bottleneck claim:

> ...mixes two conventions. The conclusion is withdrawn.

This audit reproduces the selected bit root from its complete histogram,
dimension 64, W = 28,416, and the retained `10^-16` bad-class fallback:

\[
\mathcal M_b(a)=\frac1W\sum_r n_r(r/64)^{1-a}
 +\frac{32\cdot64\cdot\#\mathrm{children}}{10^{16}W}\,64^a.
\]

Its lower endpoint is `0.001733427039589844234749`; the next `10^-24` grid point
fails. At the user's target its invoice residual is approximately
`-0.000009309331366948437182931347`.

| Bit supplier convention | Cap before strict outer gaps | Maximum gain over the user's target |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Retained PR589, repriced here | 0.001733427039589844234749 | 0.000002848602323597416009; **0.1646040574%** |
| PR3647 R437, endpoint reported by PR3700 | 0.001736628253427115480286 | 0.000006049816160868661546; **0.3495834705%** |

Therefore a modest complex gain can produce a κ gain without a bit change.
Larger gains would require bit work as well. R437's extra headroom is conditional
on its missing admission obligations and must not be silently substituted into
the selected construction.

## Exact allowance for a changed consumer

Assume the involutory word really replaces eight H8 children by eight H1
children on each of the 3,240 calls, while all inherited physical costs stay
accounted for. Let `H_r(a)=r^(1-a)`. Per nine-stage replica the local saving is
`72(H8-H1)`. The maximum **additional complete consumer bill** at target `a` is

\[
B(a)=72(H_8(a)-H_1(a))
 +\frac{W\,73^{1-a}-\sum_r n_rH_r(a)}{360}.
\]

The second term charges the actual baseline invoice at the same exponent. It
cannot be dropped when comparing against the unattained ideal target.

| Test saving | Budget in H1 units | Maximum H8 fields | Maximum H7 fields |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| User target | 501.906957252301 | 62.964548980198 | 71.942857601067 |
| Retained bit cap | 501.379002090980 | 62.898689246756 | 71.867579388912 |
| Reported R437 cap | 500.785703201417 | 62.824677286460 | 71.782983183274 |

Thus the report's whole-field H8 consumer fails even its optimistic 69.75-field
rank lower bound. The exact positive-exponent allowance is below 63, not merely
63. At the user's target, that lower-bound consumer has normalized invoice
residual at least `+0.0008331047208512263`; integer 70-field H8 repair gives
`+0.0008637992435299664`.

A useful numerical threshold for a **different, genuinely constructed**
consumer is now available: 71 rank-7 operations per replica would fit the
complete arithmetic at the user's target and at either bit cap in the table.
72 rank-7 operations do not fit. At the user target their normalized residuals
are respectively `-0.0001013153839831915` and `+0.000006140274080669`.
These are hypothetical tariffs, not evidence that an H7 observation contract
exists. All acquisition, interstage movement, owned state, cleanup and return
costs must fit inside the same B(a).

If terminal repair really needs at least 69.75 full fields, its average complete
tariff at the user's target must be below approximately
`7.195798670283886` H1 units per field. This quantifies the needed interface
change. It does not replace a proof of what those observations can recover.

The zero-consumer-cost optimistic complex root is bracketed at
`0.004456548235640913439634` to `0.004456548235640913439635`.
That number is not a candidate κ. It measures the size of the local allowance
that the failed consumer spends.

## Replay and open next questions

### New finite symmetric extended contract

`symmetric_extended.py` constructs a stronger finite component on both actual
PR3734 words. Sixteen existing I-to-FULL helpers become additional source ports,
and sixteen more become additional target ports. The remaining 376 helpers are
still arbitrary and are restored. The complete independent input count remains
632; there are also 91 owned zero buffers.

An invertible Gaussian-dyadic helper basis change turns the old dirty query
matrix H into `[4 I8, 8 i I8]` on the sixteen additional source ports. Write
`Q=[R,4 I8,8 i I8]`. Since `R R^T=56 I8`, this gives `Q Q^T=8 I8` and

\[
D=I-\tfrac14 Q^TQ,\qquad D^T=D,\qquad D^2=I.
\]

Here transpose is the complex bilinear transpose, not the conjugate transpose.
The cancellation uses `i^2=-1`; no square root or odd-denominator division is
introduced. The normalization and its inverse use explicit scalar row gates
only where all involved helper frames are I or FULL.

After removing all eight centre operations, the new 128-port pair has exact
logical contracts

\[
F:(z,w)\longmapsto(Dz,w+Dz),\qquad
B:(z,w)\longmapsto(z-w,Dw).
\]

Four full 632-column sparse Gaussian replays pass, one for each provider and
orientation. Every added scalar operation is checked against the literal common
physical frame. The local profile becomes
`{1:2440,2:725,3:224,4:102,5:20}`, rank mass **5070**. No target repair child is
required. Conservative scalar invoices are 172250 forward and 181578 backward;
the scalar triangle bounds are 124 and 128 bits, below the retained 192-bit
scalar cap. These scalar checks do not discharge the inherited whole recursive
precision and recovery theorem.

This is the leading changed local contract in this follow-up. Its sixteen new
port labels are zero: their entrance frame is I and both source and target exit
frames are FULL. They therefore do not satisfy the old norm-one port geometry.
The ordinary nine-stage m=73 controller and the T360 owner allocation cannot be
reused just by increasing the port count. A complete geometry, decoder and paid
physical allocation remain necessary.

### Exact complete budget for the zero-repair component

`complete_contract_budget.py` prices a precisely stated hypothesis: keep the
original m=73 denominator and every inherited cost, remove all eight H8 centres
per call, and then pay every additional consumer cost. This hypothesis gives a
per-replica allowance of **573.906957252301 H1**, or
**71.996995056245 H8**, at the user's target. A fully constructed 71-H8 consumer
would fit both that target and the reported R437 bit cap. A 72-H8 consumer gives
back the original baseline and fails the target.

If the consumer instead reserves previously productive families and does not
return their correct transform, the productive denominator must decrease. The
zero-repair allowance is only **7.920329616837 C73 families per replica**.
Reserving eight such families already fails, even with no additional recursive
operations. Eight new auxiliary C73 invoices fail by the same numerator budget.
This is a conditional pricing model; it does not assert that every possible
retained record requires C73. A valid changed boundary contract that still
delivers every correctly transformed productive field avoids that particular
loss, but must establish its endpoint.

The general test is especially simple in normalized units: added child cost per
replica minus newly correct productive families per replica must be smaller
than `7.9203296168372553305...`, under the unchanged m=73 hypothesis. Any changed
dimension or child inventory must be repriced directly.

The concrete general-purpose terminal decoder that flattens all 896 data fields
at one H1 each is excluded exactly. Its 896 added rank units exceed the deleted
576 centre units by 320, precisely the original rank deficit per replica. Its
complete moment equals one at a=0 and is strictly increasing for every a>0.
Its root is **zero**, not a small positive improvement.

An additional planning screen considers an **unconstructed** mixed geometry
with 112 norm-one and sixteen zero-label ports, m=81, W=1024, nine calls, and
assumed paid coverage mass 82048 before its consumer. The local profile leaves
36418 units of idle rank mass. If every idle child has rank at most 57, charging
all that mass fractionally at H57 is an optimistic lower bound on its cost for
positive a. Directed rational enclosures give the following roots of this
optimistic price:

| Assumed complete terminal tariff | Optimistic root bracket |
| --- | --- |
| 70 H8 | `[0.001740857906156611936202, 0.001740857906156611936203]` |
| 71 H8 | `[0.001699376924679389803093, 0.001699376924679389803094]` |
| 72 H8 | `[0.001657897500848277472216, 0.001657897500848277472217]` |

Thus even 71 H8 would miss the user's target under those assumed coverage and
rank-cap conditions. A 70-H8 consumer leaves only an optimistic normalized
allowance of approximately `0.000023997193111267` for inefficiency above the
fractional ideal. The coverage rule, physical allocation, native controller,
and terminal decoder in this screen have not been constructed; none of these
three roots is a candidate κ.
