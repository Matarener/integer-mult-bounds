## Result and its status

The proposed conditional bound is

\[
T(n)=O\!\left(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa}\right),\qquad
\kappa=\frac{23}{10000}=0.0023.
\]

This exceeds the user's comparison point
`0.001730970419199163702738` by approximately **32.8734% in the exponent
saving**. It is not a measured runtime improvement.

The package supplies complete finite native mixed-job words, a constructive
finite compiler to ordinary uniform suppliers, the delayed BIT cover, new
precision and storage constants, and a strict outer accounting point. A
separately implemented audit reviews the changed interfaces and checks their
quantitative inequalities. The fresh replay regenerates the critical chain
from the attached base plus the preserved cumulative work.

**This is a conditional mathematical research result awaiting external
review.** It is not a formal proof certificate or an unconditional integer
multiplication theorem. The inherited analytic decomposition, native ordered
movement, fixed-tape execution, prime padding and exact-recovery hypotheses
remain. The new universal arguments are written proofs supported by finite
checks; passing Python checks does not formally verify those arguments.

The winning uniform words are specified by finite algorithms at explicit
astronomical ranks. Their instruction lists, complete covers and numerical
constants have **not** been materialized. This limitation matters for both
reproducibility expectations and any practical interpretation of the bound.

## What changed enough to escape the previous family

The decisive change is to account for the actual useful transforms delivered
on all independent inputs. A helper that returns with a known completed
transform has performed useful work. If its original input was arbitrary and
its endpoint is autonomous, that job can participate in the recursion. It
does not have to be restored to the identity merely to fit a uniform local
interface.

That observation alone is insufficient: these jobs have different ranks,
their data exist at different times, and their coordinate frames differ.
The construction therefore has three linked parts.

1. **A complete mixed-job controller.** The selected seven-call controller
   schedules the useful helper jobs in four legal idle windows. Two other
   windows are eliminated by an exact boundary factorization. Input/output
   normalizers, physical phases, source complements and ownership are explicit.

2. **An integer compiler to a uniform supplier.** Exact whole-array batches
   are routed through a causal dependency graph. Each original source also
   receives its paid complementary transform. After recursively compiling
   every smaller job, all outputs are full transforms of the same rank and
   only rank-one paid leaves remain. No fractional array or copied input is
   credited as an extra independent output.

3. **A compatible BIT interface and full outer bill.** The entire finite BIT
   program is compiled before introducing one ambient cover and one common
   external weight. The new cover, fallback, routes, row stock and precision
   are charged before the suppliers enter the multiplication accounting.

The finite compiler uses the mixed-target lockstep flow in
[Jacob Alan Sussman's Theorem 1G / PR4268](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/4268).
That upstream contribution explicitly concerns a DFT research exponent and
does not itself claim a new kappa. The additional native, finite-template and
outer transfer arguments are supplied here. The pinned upstream proof,
implementation and license are retained under `architecture/sources/`.

## Why the infinite-size statement has a finite construction

[FINITE-PRECOMPILATION.md](architecture/FINITE-PRECOMPILATION.md) gives the
integer compiler. It clears every batch denominator, uses distinct physical
input slots, executes child batches before their parents resume, and charges
the complementary axes of each source. Its exact per-source leaf recurrence
is controlled by a strict potential inequality.

[EXPLICIT-TEMPLATE-BOUNDS.md](controller/EXPLICIT-TEMPLATE-BOUNDS.md) replaces
an open-ended search for a successful rank with the following exact choices:

| --- | --- | ---: |
| Complex | `2^276540184361` | 0.002304 |
| BIT before covering | `2^6552928412` | 0.002760 |

Those are deliberately loose sufficient ranks. The proof defines a finite
program at each rank by exact integer recursion. It fixes that program before
the multiplication input length varies. Its productive width, owned storage,
scalar operations and routing count are all finite computable constants.
They are not zero, small, or experimentally measured.

The materialized rank-57 complex and rank-50 BIT examples check ownership,
batching and full-job completion. **Neither example achieves the limiting
uniform saving above.** Conflating those small examples with the winning
astronomical templates would overstate the evidence.

## Precision, storage and all nine outer costs

The new template cannot inherit the small numerical constants of the old
supplier. [FINITE-TEMPLATE-CLOSURE.md](transfer-audit/FINITE-TEMPLATE-CLOSURE.md)
defines replacements from its actual finite counts. For template rank `N`,
productive width `W`, paid leaves `P`, unit count `U` and owned count `O=U`,
one conservative choice is

```
Z = W+O
G = 1494376*U
E = 64*(Z+N+P+G+1)^3
B = P+E
C0 = 32*N*B^2
```

The completed-child semantic recurrence bounds the precision guard by
`C0*d`: its degree in the active dimension remains one. Children return
completed values on one fine grid. The proof does not count unfinished
child temporaries as simultaneous unfinished parent operands.

BIT's cover prime obeys `q > 25,600,000*N^5` and avoids every fixed rational
denominator, determinant and rank witness. Its bad-class fallback is fully
paid. Its new row stock is carried into the global support argument. A
conservative simultaneous reserve is

```
q0(h,p) <= C*(log2(h) + h^0.003*(log2(p))^3 + 1).
```

Reserved selected bits are deferred to later shrinking phases. They are not
first transformed individually under an obsolete reserve bound. The remaining
tail is polylogarithmic; row padding is removed after every completed phase.

## Further rounds toward 0.004 and beyond

The research continued after the complete identity construction was found.
The larger H branch has useful exact primitives, but it does not establish
a higher kappa in this checkpoint.

- **Cheaper BIT local responses.** An involution completion replaces nine
  rank-seven corrections by nine rank-one corrections. A further exact probe
  observes that the uncorrected map has corank three and constructs a minimum
  three-row invertible completion. An independent audit checks all four bare
  and wrapped native variants on all 661 arbitrary columns. Invertibility is sufficient when the caller uses
  the appropriate inverse; an involution is a stronger restriction than needed.
- **Correct complex owner phases.** A signed monomial corrects an owner map
  that preserved the polar form but had the wrong Gaussian phase. All 896
  recipes and 137,984 quadratic tests pass an independent implementation.
  Its column lift retains the scalar `i^f`.
- **A complete boundary obstruction.** The noncommuting controller leaves
  three interval endpoint corrections. Direct completion costs 672 rank-one
  requests in complex arithmetic and 666 in BIT. The proposed single shared
  head correction is not established. Even the three-row BIT completion has
  rank-one surplus `645-666=-21` after direct normalization.
- **Additional cancellation probes.** A universal model with Q-free mixing
  and only two whole-bank Q passages has coefficient span at most one; the
  actual three interval words have rank three. A concrete signed-owner
  witness also rejects cancellation by fresh-axis inverse stages. These
  obstructions have explicit scopes and do not rule out geometry-aware coding.

The strongest next H question is whether the actual query subspaces permit
a joint boundary decoder that shares part of those three corrections. A
free noncommutative cancellation argument is too coarse. Any next probe
should emit the native queries, preserve the input ownership and sign data,
and include preparation, routes, reads, restoration and endpoint completion
in the same invoice.

The tested identity menus also include controllers through p6, 64 p3 masks,
123,904 commuting stage allocations and 96,141 BIT helper pairs. Their
failures are retained as finite negative results, with no global optimality
claim. [LITERATURE-AND-PROBES.md](LITERATURE-AND-PROBES.md) records the primary
sources, the applicable ideas, what was implemented, and the changes of
direction.
