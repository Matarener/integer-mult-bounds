# Singular reachability over coefficient domains

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. Apache-2.0.

Let K be any field and R any commutative integral domain containing K.
There are exactly two data roles. A paid factor is `I+u v^T`, where
`u in R^2` and `v in K^2`. Any `GL_2(K)` factor is free, anywhere in a
finite word. There are no extra roles. Multiplication is ordinary matrix
multiplication over R. Reachability means equality of the complete matrix,
so every input column is covered. It does not mean a reversible dirty
workspace contract or a native implementation.

## Theorem

For a matrix M in `M_2(R)` with determinant zero, the following are equivalent:

1. M is reachable by a finite word in this alphabet.
2. M factors as `M=a b`, with `a in R^2` a column and `b in R^(1 by 2)` a row.
3. M is reachable with at most three paid factors.

In particular a singular target is either reachable within three paid factors
or unreachable at every finite length. This is a reachability classification,
not an exact classification of the minimum within the reachable cases.

### Necessity

The determinant identity for a paid factor N is

```text
det(I+u v^T) = 1+v^T u.
```

The determinant of a free factor is nonzero. Since R is an integral domain,
a word with determinant zero must contain a paid factor N with determinant
zero. Then `v != 0` and

```text
v^T N = (1+v^T u) v^T = 0.
```

The kernel of the nonzero constant row `v^T` is a free rank-one R-module
generated by a constant column. This requires only division by a nonzero
element of K. Explicitly, if `v1 != 0`, take

```text
a0 = (-v2/v1, 1)^T,     b0 = row 2 of N.
```

Each column of N is in that kernel, so `N=a0 b0`. If `v1=0`, then `v2 != 0`
and `N=e1 (row 1 of N)`. Write the whole word as `M=A N B`, with A and B
the products on its two sides, including all free factors. Therefore

```text
M = (A a0) (b0 B).
```

Both factors have entries in R. Neither A nor B needs to be invertible.
This proves necessity without bounding the length or assuming a special
position for the singular factor.

### Sufficiency

We use the outer-product constructor of Nick Petalas's #4808, cited rather
than claimed as new. For any a and b over R,

```text
[[a1,0],[a2,1]] [[1,b2],[0,0]] [[b1,0],[0,1]] = a b.
```

The three right vectors are respectively `e1,e2,e1`; hence these are legal
paid factors. This also handles zero factors. The identity gives (2) => (3),
and (3) => (1) is immediate. The new direction is the necessity above.

## An all-field unreachable singular target

Take `R=K[s^2,st,t^2]`, the indicated subring of the polynomial domain
`K[s,t]`, and

```text
M = [[s^2,st],[st,t^2]].
```

Its determinant is zero, and its rank over the fraction field is one.
Nevertheless it has no outer-product factorization over R.

Suppose `M=a b`. All four entries of a and b are nonzero, because all four
entries of M are nonzero. In the polynomial UFD `K[s,t]`, the entry a1
divides both `s^2` and `st`. Thus a1 divides their gcd s, so a1 is a nonzero
constant or a nonzero constant times s. Every polynomial in R has only
monomials of even total degree, so the second case is impossible. Write
`a1=c in K^*`. Then `b1=s^2/c`, while `a2 b1=st` would require

```text
a2 = c t/s,
```

which is not in `K[s,t]`. This is a contradiction. The proof works in every
characteristic. By the theorem, M is unreachable at every finite paid length,
even with free `GL_2(K)` factors anywhere.

The polynomial UFD fact and gcd computation are classical. The new result
is the right-vector reachability criterion and its consequence for the
explicit open target in #4808. No general ring-theory priority is claimed.

## Why the hypotheses matter

Over the larger coefficient domain `K[s,t]`, the same target is
`(s,t)^T (s,t)` and is reachable within three paid factors by the cited word.
The missing coefficients s and t matter. Every scalar specialization of
s and t is also an outer product over K. Such finite evaluations cannot
prove the polynomial obstruction.

The domain hypothesis is used to obtain a determinant-zero factor from a
determinant-zero product. No version for rings with zero divisors is asserted.
The proof uses exactly two roles and constant right vectors; additional
restored roles or a different alphabet require a separate argument.
