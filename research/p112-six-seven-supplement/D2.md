## Theorem and scope

For t=2 or t=3, if an actual restricted dual is a nonzero multiple of a
live root, **I>=65**, hence I-epsilon_k>=64 for epsilon_k in {0,1}.
The additional marked witness g is unnecessary for this invoice theorem.
Without a root mark, **I>=64**. Under the exact actual-rank and nonparallel
hypotheses in D1 Section 8, the singleton-pivot consumer therefore has
**cost at least 64** also when its nonpivot low space has d=2.

No N8, minimum actual basis, actual adaptation to T, distinct corrected low
frame indices, or completeness of historical normalized lists is assumed. The
result is the complete stated d=2 invoice, not complete C1 or a global decoder
normalization theorem. No sharpness at64 is claimed.

The internal budgeted lemma is stronger: for d=2, the assumptions sum_F w<=72
(t2), or <=73 (t3), already imply I>=65, with no root mark. The proof below
derives T's dimension directly and exhausts its minimum profiles using
elementary inequalities and the intrinsic exclusions.

## Restricted four-axis rigidity

A nonroot/frame normal supported on at most four frame axes has at least 76
live nonzeros. The one-, two- and three-axis cases are already in D1: one is a
frame axis; two nonzero axes give76 or88; three give>=80.

For four nonzero coefficients, the four selected axes either form an incidence
block or do not. G5's S(3,4,8) incidence gives the following restricted forms
and multiplicities.

* Block: six signed pairs a_i +/- a_j, each form with multiplicity8;
  eight projective four-sign forms, each with multiplicity1; eight zero
  sites. If all four coefficients agree up to signs, this is a root
  direction. Otherwise at most three pairs agree up to sign, so the
  pair forms alone contribute >=8(12-3)=72. Pairing four-sign forms by
  changing the sign of one nonzero coefficient shows that at least four
  survive, hence >=76 in total.
* Nonblock: each singleton has multiplicity8; each signed pair has
  multiplicity4; each projective signed triple has multiplicity2. The
  singletons contribute32, pairs contribute>=24, and each of four
  triples has at most one vanishing sign form, contributing>=6. Thus
  the total is>=80.

All statements use equality up to sign over characteristic zero, with no
ordering or absolute-value operation on the field. Consequently a normal with
<=4 active axes and <=75 live nonzeros is a root/frame.

## Three seven-coordinate kernels and A2 planes

In a minimum profile 777c (c<=3), put D=C intersect T, dim D=2. Restrict the
three seven-piece coordinate functionals to D. Any pair has zero joint kernel:
otherwise a codeword is supported on one seven-piece and the small piece, at
most 40 sites. Thus their three kernel lines are nonzero and distinct. Their
codewords are supported on two seven-pieces and the small support.

Three distinct root lines in a normal plane form A2. An A2 plane has active
frame support either four (if it contains a frame root) or six. Indeed
nonframe roots use four-axis blocks. Distinct blocks of a nonorthogonal root
pair overlap in two axes, whose pairings must align, and their third root uses
the symmetric difference; the union has six axes. In the same block a
nonorthogonal pair gives a frame root. An A2 plane with <=5 active frame axes
therefore contains a frame root f0 and has support one four-axis block.

Write such a plane as span(f0,v), with v a nonframe root in that block. Its
f0-coordinate kernel is v-(v,f0)f0/2, a nonroot with three active axes. The
other three active frame-coordinate kernels are all the same line K f0.
Therefore among a family with distinct ROOT coordinate-kernel lines, at most
one can be corrected with a frame row.
