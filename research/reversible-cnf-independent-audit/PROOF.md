## 1. The precise decision problem

There are five independent, arbitrarily initialized roles. A role holds
\((\mathbb C^{2^n})^{\otimes f}\), where \(n\ge1\) is unbounded and
\(f\ge1\). The only native generators in this restricted class are

\[
 Z_j e_x=(-1)^{x_j}e_x.
\]

Permitted instructions are rational ADD and QUERY between distinct roles,
unary APPLY of a \(Z_j\), and role SWAP. A query adds a rational multiple
of \(Z_j^{\otimes f}\) of the current source to the current destination.
All rational numerators and denominators and variable indices have
ordinary finite binary encodings. The decision problem is equality of
the complete five-role endpoints of two words, for all dirty inputs and
all positive widths. The hardness construction uses the empty word as
reference. It has no SCALE, CLEAR, assumed zero role, or discarded return.

The signs on a tensor basis tuple \((x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(f)})\) are

\[
 z_j=(-1)^{\sum_t x_j^{(t)}}\in\{1,-1\}.
 \tag{1}
\]

No instruction changes its tuple of payload addresses. Restricting the
complete word to such a tuple therefore gives an ordinary \(5\times5\)
rational matrix, obtained by substituting these signs. Every assignment
of the signs is realized at width one, and at any larger positive width
by adding zero addresses. Hence complete endpoint equality in this
class is exactly equality of those matrices at every sign assignment.
This observation concerns commuting sign generators only; it is not an
extension to arbitrary native or independent operators.

## 2. The controlled swap on two arbitrary inputs

Let \(z\) be an involution, initially either the scalar sign in (1) or
the operator \(Z_j^{\otimes f}\). Define its complementary projectors
\(p_+=(1+z)/2\) and \(p_-=(1-z)/2\). Since \(z^2=1\),

\[
 p_+^2=p_+,\quad p_-^2=p_-,\quad p_+p_-=0,\quad p_++p_-=1.
\]

The source's five chronological steps on arbitrary \(x,y\) are

\[
 x\leftarrow x-y,\quad
 y\leftarrow y+x/2,\quad
 y\leftarrow y-zx/2,\quad
 x\leftarrow zx,\quad
 x\leftarrow x+y.
 \tag{2}
\]

After the first step set \(a=x_{\rm old}-y_{\rm old}\). The next
two steps give \(y_{\rm new}=y_{\rm old}+(1-z)a/2\). The final
two give \(x_{\rm new}=za+y_{\rm new}\). Thus the full endpoint is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}x_{\rm new}\\y_{\rm new}\end{pmatrix}
 =S(z)\begin{pmatrix}x_{\rm old}\\y_{\rm old}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 S(z)=\begin{pmatrix}p_+&p_-\\p_-&p_+\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{3}
\]

In the commutative ring generated by \(z\),
\(S(z)^2=I\) and \(\det S(z)=p_+^2-p_-^2=z\). At sign \(+1\)
the endpoint is the identity; at sign \(-1\) it is the swap. Both
initial values remain independent throughout the derivation: setting
either input to zero is unnecessary and would not check the claimed
dirty-input contract. The other three roles are unchanged.

Each ADD or QUERY in (2) is a distinct-role shear, with inverse obtained
by negating its coefficient. APPLY of \(z\) is its own inverse. Thus
every individual instruction is invertible, not merely the completed
two-role map. The only nonintegral coefficients are \(1/2,-1/2\).

The source's serialized generator has identity affine matrix rows,
zero offset and constant phase, zero quadratic rows, and linear phase
coefficient two at coordinate \(j\). Its literal input phase is
\(i^{2x_j}=(-1)^{x_j}\), so it is precisely the required \(Z_j\),
including at tensor width \(f\) via (1).

## 3. Chronological permutations and lowering a branch

Represent a permutation by its five images, with
\((pq)(i)=p(q(i))\): the rightmost map acts first. A chronological
list \(g_1,\ldots,g_L\) therefore has endpoint
\(g_L\cdots g_1\). This is the convention in the source's
`compose` and `evaluate`.

A cycle \((i,j_1,\ldots,j_{s-1})\) is implemented by the chronological
swaps \((i,j_1),(i,j_2),\ldots,(i,j_{s-1})\). Their product sends
\(i\) to \(j_1\), each \(j_t\) to \(j_{t+1}\), and the last
element back to \(i\). This proves the order used by `swaps`, not
only the unordered transposition decomposition. Disjoint cycles need
at most \(5-\text{number of cycles}\le4\) swaps in total.

For a branch selecting \(a\) at bit zero and \(b\) at bit one,
`lower_word` first performs \(a\), then performs the swaps for
\(ba^{-1}\), with each swap replaced by (2). The controlling sign
is \(z=(-1)^{\text{bit}}\). At bit zero the second part is identity;
at bit one the endpoint is \((ba^{-1})a=b\). Thus both branches
act on every dirty column with exactly the specified permutation.
The cost is at most \(4+5\cdot4=24\) register instructions. A
constant branch has \(a=b\) and needs only the first permutation;
the same upper bound remains valid.

`invert_word` reverses the chronological list and replaces both branch
permutations by their inverses. On each assignment its endpoint is
therefore the inverse of the original endpoint. Inverting individual
permutations without reversing the list would not establish this fact.

## 4. Boolean compilation and the commutator convention

Use the source's three permutations

\[
 A=(1,2,3,4,0),\quad B=(1,3,0,4,2),\quad
 C=B^{-1}A^{-1}BA=(4,3,1,0,2).
 \tag{4}
\]

Each is a five-cycle. For any five-cycle target \(T\), the compiler
maintains the invariant: its word is identity when the Boolean formula
is false and \(T\) when the formula is true.

For a positive literal, a single branch selects identity or \(T\)
at its variable's zero or one value. A negative literal reverses those
branches. Boolean false is the empty word, and Boolean true is one
constant \(T\) branch. The implementation distinguishes Boolean
constants from integer literal indices before testing the integer case.

For NOT, invert the child word and append the constant \(T\) branch.
If its child endpoint is \(P\), the chronological endpoint is
\(TP^{-1}\), which is \(T\) for \(P=I\) and \(I\) for \(P=T\).

For AND, construct \(F\) by
\(F(C^j(0))=T^j(0)\), for \(0\le j<5\). Both cycles visit all five
points, so this defines a permutation and proves \(FCF^{-1}=T\).
Compile the children with targets \(FAF^{-1}\) and \(FBF^{-1}\).
If their endpoints are \(P,Q\), the chronological list

\[
 \text{word}(P),\ \text{word}(Q),\
 \text{word}(P)^{-1},\ \text{word}(Q)^{-1}
\]

has endpoint **\(Q^{-1}P^{-1}QP\)**. If either child is false, the
nonidentity factors cancel. If both are true, this endpoint is
\(F(B^{-1}A^{-1}BA)F^{-1}=T\), using exactly (4). This checks the
chronological order in the source; a different convention for a
commutator cannot silently be substituted. OR is compiled as
\(\neg(\neg P\wedge\neg Q)\), so the same invariant proves it.

Induction establishes the invariant for every formula constructed by
the compiler, including repeated and negated literals, tautological
clauses, and the Boolean constants. In particular an empty clause is
false, while an empty conjunction of clauses is true. With root target
\(A\), the CNF endpoint is exactly \(A\) on satisfying assignments
and identity on all others.

## 5. The three size bounds and what they count

Let \(w(F)\) count group-program branch instructions. A literal has
length one, NOT changes length \(s\) to \(s+1\), AND of lengths
\(s,t\) has length \(2s+2t\), and the displayed OR construction
has length \(2s+2t+5\). A clause with one, two, or three literals
therefore has length at most \(1,9,25\), respectively. An empty
clause has length zero. These are bounds for the actual syntactic
construction; they do not depend on simplification of the formula.

For \(m\ge1\) clauses, `balanced` pairs adjacent nodes and carries
an unmatched last node to the next level. It takes at most
\(\lceil\log_2m\rceil\) rounds. Each AND ancestor duplicates a
clause word twice, so its total multiplicity is at most
\(2^{\lceil\log_2m\rceil}\). Consequently

\[
 w(\text{CNF})\le25m\,2^{\lceil\log_2m\rceil}\le50m^2.
 \tag{5}
\]

For \(m=0\), the empty conjunction compiles to one constant branch,
which also satisfies \(50\max(1,m)^2\). Applying the 24-instruction
branch lowering gives

\[
 \text{register instructions}\le1200\max(1,m)^2.
 \tag{6}
\]

This is an instruction-count bound. To obtain a polynomial-time
many-one reduction with ordinary input encodings, first relabel the
distinct variables actually appearing in the 3-CNF as \(1,\ldots,q\).
There are at most \(3m\); choose \(n=\max(1,q)\). This renaming
preserves satisfiability and is polynomial in the original binary
input length, including when the old variable names are very large.

The source `lower_word` emits all \(n\) generators, each with dense
length-\(n\) recipe tables. There are \(O(n^2)\) integer entries,
but each identity matrix row `1 << j` has \(j+1\) bits. Repeating
those rows in all generators gives \(O(n^3)\) bits in the explicit
tables, rather than \(O(n^2)\) bits. The word serialization adds
\(O(\max(1,m)^2\log(n+1))\) bits, since roles and coefficients
have constant size and generator indices have logarithmic size.
With the renaming above, both output length and construction time are
polynomial. Equations (5)–(6) do not claim a quadratic byte bound for
an arbitrary binary-declared \(n\) with many unused axes.

## 6. coNP membership with an explicit bit bound

For two input words, let \(S\) be the set of generator indices
actually referenced in either word. Assign one bit to each element
of \(S\), and identify a differing row/column of their five-role
endpoints. This is a polynomial-size certificate of inequality.
Only referenced generators require signs: with \(L\) total
instructions, \(|S|\le L\). If \(n\) is given compactly in binary,
there is no need to write a full \(n\)-bit assignment to unused axes.
Conceptually, put zeros on those axes to realize the supplied signs
as a payload address. The certificate verifier works with signs and
does not construct that potentially huge dense address.

Exact endpoint evaluation also has polynomial bit complexity. Normalize
each rational shear coefficient as \(p_t/q_t\), where \(q_t>0\).
At a fixed sign assignment its elementary matrix \(M_t\) has
\(N_t=q_tM_t\) integral and

\[
 \|N_t\|_\infty\le q_t+|p_t|,
\]

where the norm is the maximum absolute row sum. For an APPLY or SWAP
take \(q_t=1\) and bound \(\|N_t\|_\infty\le1\). At every
prefix, the endpoint has the unreduced representation

\[
 M_s\cdots M_1
  =\frac{N_s\cdots N_1}{\prod_{t=1}^s q_t},\qquad
 \|N_s\cdots N_1\|_\infty
  \le\prod_{t=1}^s\|N_t\|_\infty.                  \tag{7}
\]

Thus denominator bit length is at most the sum of denominator bit
lengths, and numerator bit length is at most
\(1+\sum_t\lceil\log_2\max(1,q_t+|p_t|)\rceil\), taking zero
contribution for the unit-norm steps. This is bounded by the total
coefficient encoding length plus \(O(L)\). Reduction of fractions
can only shorten the resulting numerator and denominator. The same
bound holds for every prefix; comparing the two final rational
matrices by cross multiplication adds their bit bounds. Constant-size
matrix arithmetic on these integers takes polynomial time.

By Section 1, a true endpoint discrepancy occurs at some assignment
of the referenced signs, and conversely any certified matrix
discrepancy is a genuine dirty-input discrepancy at width one. This
proves that the complement of the equality problem is in NP, hence
the equality problem is in coNP. The source's stated bit bound is
valid; (7) makes the denominator and magnitude accounting explicit.

## 7. Hardness and the implementation boundary

Start with the classical coNP-complete problem of 3-CNF
unsatisfiability, perform the variable renaming in Section 5, and
emit the source compiler's five-role word with empty reference.
Sections 2–4 show that on each sign assignment its full endpoint is
identity or the nonidentity five-cycle \(A\), according as the
formula is false or true. Every assignment is realized by a tensor
basis address. Therefore

\[
 \text{CNF is unsatisfiable}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{compiled word equals the empty reference on all dirty inputs
 and all positive widths}.                           \tag{8}
\]

A satisfying assignment produces a differing role endpoint; it is
not merely a difference on an auxiliary register that the contract
allows one to ignore. The construction has exactly five arbitrary
input roles, returns all five, and every emitted step is invertible.
The size and encoding discussion proves that (8) is a polynomial-time
many-one reduction. Together with Section 6, this establishes the
source's coNP-completeness assertion for its stated unbounded class.

The predecessor's command-line implementation limits axes to 256 and
has separate role and stored-term limits. Those are explicitly
distinct from the unbounded theorem. We do not ascribe the unbounded
complexity classification to a fixed-axis finite implementation, and
we do not treat a stored-term resource failure as an equality decision.
No physical native cost, multiplication bound, or fast general solver
follows from the reduction.
