## Support inequality and arbitrary dirty banks

Let h be the maximum row support of the cumulative coefficient matrix on
all current original and auxiliary bank/address coordinates. Initially
h=1 on identity rows (zero rows, if separately permitted, do not hurt).
An address/phase monomial, scalar multiplication or bank permutation does
not increase h. A native rank-r macro has exactly2^r entries per row, so
its output row is a sum of at most2^r prior rows and h increases by at
most that factor. A counted binary ADD or target accumulation combines
two previous rows and increases h by at most2. Arbitrary coefficients or
cancellation only reduce support relative to this union bound.

Thus every output row of any proposed replacement has support at most
`2^(R_f+S_f)`, where R_f is the sum of actual native macroarities and S_f
counts every elementary binary ADD/COPY accumulation. The required WR
block has exactly `2^(6f)` or `2^(8f)` entries in every row: its exact
native/monomial/native-adapter witness preserves row support. Inputs of
other banks occupy disjoint coefficient columns, so they cannot cancel
this required independent-input block. Additional arbitrary dirty banks
only add initial identity columns; restoring them cannot remove the old
input3 coefficient. Hence

    q2: R_f+S_f>=6f,       q3: R_f+S_f>=8f.

This permits arbitrary new scalar/monomial coupling topology inside this
fixed local block. It does not fix the old ADD graph or confine edits to
one row. It assumes the declared linear macro alphabet; arbitrary nonlinear
value gates or support-growing primitives with an omitted charge are not
licensed by the proof.

The exact finite-f bound is `R_f>=nf-S_f`, n=6/8, not a native-only
pointwise `R_f>=nf`. Bounded or sublinear S_f gives only
`liminf R_f/f>=n`. Together with the fixed prefixes66f/120f, that excludes
a fixed asymptotic below-stock native deficit at these entrances. It does
not prove the local optimum is6/8 or10/12.

The support count uses the SAME-VOLUME FULL-BANK native query macro tariff.
It does not identify unweighted macroarity with the cost of arbitrary
striped or partial-volume primitive queries. Such queries need their own
weighted invoice and implementation analysis. COPY is not a free fan-in
primitive; every binary target accumulation must be counted.

## Full-scan bridge

Section4 of the threshold note is correct under its explicit cost premises:
the counted operations must incur actual work
`T_scan(f)>=c V_f S_f` for fixed c>0, and the complete available overhead
must be `o(V_f f)`. Merely writing down a sequence of formal full-field
ADDs is not a lower bound for a different fused or partial-volume primitive
implementation. Here S_f counts actual elementary binary operations in the
declared macro model, with all required per-record work charged.

A fixed native shortfall `R_f<=(n-delta)f` forces `S_f>=delta f`, giving
`T_scan=Omega(V_f f)`. It cannot fit a budget
`O(V_f f^lambda P(f))` with lambda<1 and
`P(f)=o(f^(1-lambda))`. An unrestricted precision/field/controller multiplier
cannot be dropped. The threshold note explicitly leaves the changed
caller/source/precision linkage unproved; this review does too.

Under that sublinear full-scan budget S_f=o(f). For equal-volume full-bank
child normalization and p=1-b in(0,1), subadditivity gives
`sum x_i^p >= (sum x_i)^p`. The limiting cleanup moment is therefore at
least `(n/m)^p=(1/2)^p`. The actual fixed prefixes give respectively

    33(1/6)^p+(1/2)^p > 6,
    60(1/8)^p+(1/2)^p > 8.

These strict inequalities follow because at p=1 the sums are exactly
stock and every displayed fraction is below1. They exclude a favorable
native component moment for these fixed cuts under that normalization
and scan budget. They do not themselves prove a new multiplication
recurrence or a precision/tape theorem.
