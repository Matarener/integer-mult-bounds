# Cyclic classes of balanced depth-three chain schedules

Prepared by SHIN09-git with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance. Apache-2.0.
This is a scoped combinatorial proof, with bounded exact corroboration.
It is not a new multiplication bound or a supplier admission.

## Contract and credited starting point

Work over a nonzero unital ring of exact characteristic m >= 2, with four
distinct roles x,z1,z2,y, arbitrary initial data, immutable x, and arbitrary
compatible linear maps. A chronological letter means one complete call:

```text
1: z1 += B1 x       2: z2 += B2 z1       3: y += B3 z2.
```

A balanced word has m copies of each letter and length 3m. Let A(w), B(w),
T(w) count scattered 12, 23, 123 subsequences respectively. In the
predecessor's interval notation T is N13, not the count of the two-letter
subsequence 13. Nick Petalas's
[chain-tariff proof](source/chain-tariff-PROOF.md), building on #4249,
shows that the complete endpoint is correct for target c precisely when

```text
A(w) = B(w) = 0 (mod m),       T(w) = c (mod m).
```

Indeed the other three interval counts equal m. The interval expansion
gives every coefficient of every arbitrary dirty input, without commuting
the maps. We allow c=0 for the counting identities; it does not represent a
shortest nonzero-target connector. Write W(m,c) for the number of these
literal balanced words, with 0 <= c < m.

Set W(1,0)=6 as an auxiliary combinatorial convention: all permutations of
123 meet vacuous congruences. We do not posit a nonzero ring of characteristic
one. This convention is necessary when a length-3m word repeats a length-3
block m times.

Cyclic rotation preserves correctness. This observation is already in
Petalas's [depth-five note](source/depth5-README.md), and is **not new here**.
One can either conjugate the central unitriangular endpoint by its first
generator, or move a leading letter to the end. A leading 1 changes A by -m
and T by -B; a leading 2 changes (A,B) by (m,-m) and leaves T unchanged; a
leading 3 changes B by m and T by A. All changes vanish modulo m at a correct
endpoint.

The new application below classifies the rotation stabilizers at depth three.
Burnside's lemma and divisor Möbius inversion are classical tools, restated
with their counting arguments; no novelty is claimed for those tools.

## 1. Exact repetition law

Let d divide m and n=m/d. Let v have n copies of each letter, and put
a=A(v), b=B(v), t=T(v). Partition a subsequence according to the copies of v
containing its letters. Within one copy there are d choices; within two
ordered distinct copies there are binomial(d,2) choices; within three
distinct copies there are binomial(d,3) choices. Therefore, over the integers,

```text
A(v^d) = d a + binomial(d,2) n^2,
B(v^d) = d b + binomial(d,2) n^2,
T(v^d) = d t + binomial(d,2) n (a+b) + binomial(d,3) n^3.     (1)
```

The two arrangements using exactly two copies contribute a*n and n*b.

**Pair gate.** If d is even and n is odd, the pair shift is
(d/2)(d-1)n^2, which is d/2 modulo d. Neither pair count can be divisible
by dn. Otherwise (d-1)n is even, and division by d makes the pair shift
((d-1)n/2)n, a multiple of n. Thus both pair conditions hold exactly when

```text
(d is odd or n is even) and a = b = 0 (mod n).               (2)
```

Under (2), write a+b=nk. The two-copy contribution in T, divided by dn, is
(d-1)nk/2, an integer. It vanishes modulo m, leaving the target condition

```text
c = d t + binomial(d,3)n^3 (mod dn).                        (3)
```

In particular, the cubic shift cannot in general be discarded or divided
by d separately; for d=3 it need not be divisible by 3.

## 2. Fixed points, least periods and cyclic counts for every m

For d|m, define n=m/d, s=binomial(d,3)n^3, and

```text
F_d(m,c) = 0,                               if d even and n odd;
         = 0,                               if d does not divide c-s;
         = W(n, ((c-s)/d) mod n),            otherwise.     (4)
```

All quotients here are exact integers. Replacing c by c+m does not change
the formula.

**Theorem.** F_d is the number of correct words fixed by any rotation of
order d. The number E_d of correct words whose *maximal repetition factor*
is exactly d, hence whose least positive rotation period is 3m/d, is

```text
E_d(m,c) = sum_{e: d|e|m} mu(e/d) F_e(m,c).                 (5)
```

Consequently the number O of cyclic classes, and the number P of primitive
words (least period 3m), are

```text
O(m,c) = (1/(3m)) sum_{d|m} phi(d) F_d(m,c),
P(m,c) = E_1(m,c) = sum_{d|m} mu(d) F_d(m,c).               (6)
```

**Proof.** A rotation of order d partitions 3m positions into cycles of
size d. A fixed balanced word therefore requires d|m. If d does not divide
m there are no fixed balanced words, even when d divides 3m. If d|m, the
subgroup generated by the rotation is the same as that generated by the
shift 3m/d. Its fixed words are uniquely v^d, where v has n of each letter.
Equations (2)--(3) prove (4).

Every word has a unique maximal repetition factor r dividing m. It belongs
to F_d precisely when d|r, so F_d = sum_{d|r|m} E_r. For each word the
divisor sum sum_{k|r/d} mu(k) is one if r=d and zero otherwise. This proves
(5), including d=1.

The cyclic group of order 3m has phi(d) rotations of each order d|3m.
Count pairs (word, rotation fixing it). Each orbit contributes 3m such
pairs, by orbit size times stabilizer size. The rotations with d not
dividing m contribute zero. This gives (6). Equivalently
O=sum_{d|m} E_d/(3m/d). QED.

This is a transform of W, **not a closed formula for W itself**. It supplies
exact rotational deduplication counts and divisibility checks without
enumerating the length-3m words again.

## 3. Sharp restriction for unit targets

Assume 1 <= c < m and gcd(c,m)=1. Let R(m,c) count correct balanced words
with a nontrivial repetition.

**Corollary.** A nonprimitive such word has maximal repetition factor
exactly 3. It can occur only when m=3n with 3 not dividing n and
c=n^3 (mod 3). In that case, set t0=((c-n^3)/3) mod n. Then

```text
R(m,c) = W(n,t0).
```

In all other cases R(m,c)=0. Thus

```text
P(m,c) = W(m,c)-R(m,c),
O(m,c) = (W(m,c)+2R(m,c))/(3m),
W(m,c)-R(m,c) = 0 (mod 3m).                                (7)
```

For n=1 the convention W(1,0)=6 applies.

**Proof.** If a word repeats r>1 times, choose a prime p|r and view it as
u^p with m/p copies of each letter in u. For p=2, either the pair gate
fails or (3) makes c even. Both contradict correctness with a unit target.
For p>=5, all of p, binomial(p,2), binomial(p,3) are divisible by p, so (1)
makes c divisible by p. Thus r is a power of three.

If 9|m, viewing the word as u^3 makes (3) give c=0 modulo 3, because
m/3 is divisible by 3. This is impossible. Therefore 3 divides m exactly
once, and r=3. Equation (4) counts the repeated words and gives the displayed
R. The shorter target t0 is a unit modulo n: since 3 is invertible modulo n,
3t0=c (mod n). The smaller base word has no nontrivial repetition by the
same argument (3 does not divide n). Each repeated word therefore has least
period 3n=m. All remaining words have least period 3m. Counting these two
orbit sizes proves (7). The condition is necessary for repetition; it does
not guarantee W(n,t0)>0. QED.

**Existence classification.** The preceding necessary conditions are also
sufficient for a nonprimitive unit-target word, except at **(m,c)=(6,5)
and (12,1)**. Thus such a word exists if and only if

```text
m = 3n, 3 does not divide n, c = n^3 (mod 3),
and (m,c) is neither (6,5) nor (12,1).                       (8)
```

Here the standing unit-target hypothesis still applies. To prove this,
use the credited balanced existence theorem in #4249/#4257: for n>=2,
W(n,t)>0 at nonzero t except (n,t)=(2,1),(3,2),(4,3).
The corollary gives gcd(t0,n)=1 and 3 not dividing n, so the n=3 exception
cannot arise. The n=2 exception becomes m=6,c=5: the congruences are
c=2 modulo 3 and c=1 modulo 2. The n=4,t0=3 exception becomes m=12,c=1:
c=1 modulo 3 and c=3t0=1 modulo 4. The n=1 convention gives six words
and no exception. In every other allowed case the predecessor theorem
supplies a valid block v; v^3 has the required endpoint and least period m.
This is a new application of that credited existence theorem, not a new
proof of its balanced-word construction. QED.

## 4. Exact examples and boundaries

For (m,c)=(3,1), W=24. Six words are triples of permutations of 123;
they make two orbits of size 3. The other 18 words make two orbits of size 9.
There are **four** cyclic classes, not 24/9. An explicit repeated correct
word is 123123123, with integer (A,B,T)=(6,6,10).

For (4,0), W=2370: 42 words have repetition factor 2, and 2328 are
primitive. There are 42/6+2328/12=201 classes. The unit-target restriction
must therefore not be applied to arbitrary targets.

For (5,1), all 5340 words are primitive and give 356 classes. For (6,5),
the necessary threefold-repetition congruence holds but W(2,1)=0, so every
one of the 22320 words is primitive.

The high-modulus table is **derived from the frozen #6037 counts**, not
independently enumerated here. For example (15,8) has W=15776887005480630
and R=W(5,1)=5340. Equation (7) gives 350597489010918 classes. Only the
small-m counts and the general proof receive the independent checks described
in README; the larger numerical rows inherit #6037's finite census.

## Scope

Rotation equivalence concerns the universal algebraic endpoint and balanced
letter counts. It is not equivalence of physical execution time, routing,
precision, workspace occupancy, boundary availability or admission cost.
No instruction is deleted or made free. The number of calls in each literal
word remains 3m. The theorem does not establish all-m distinctness of W,
asymptotic equidistribution, a faster census, a new supplier or any new kappa.
