## I  Adaptive tails and exact completion

Let a paid inventory have positive ranks r and multiplicities P_r. For s>0 put P_s=Σ_r P_r r^s. Define T_s similarly for actual independent productive jobs. A complete first-moment margin is F(C)=T_(1−C)−P_(1−C). Owned scratch has no productive term. All old target debits and new paid jobs belong in these definitions.

### A01  A paid class can use its own tail order  E

**Theorem.** Partition the paid jobs into disjoint, fully declared classes E_j. For t>0, threshold J>0 and arbitrary u_j>0,

    Σ_(r>J) P_r r^t ≤ Σ_j J^(−u_j) Σ_(r in E_j) P_r r^(t+u_j).

**Proof.** On a tail job r>J, (r/J)^u_j>1. Multiply by its nonnegative P_r r^t, sum within its unique class, and finally include the nonnegative non-tail terms on the right. No statistical independence is used. QED.

**Use.** Classify actual jobs by origin, such as centre, proper transition, boundary or restoration. A compiler proof may use different orders for those declared classes while keeping the same physical word. Its exact tail charge must still be derived from its own completion contract.

### A02  Optimizing class orders weakly improves a common order  E

**Theorem.** For finite order menus U_j that all contain u0, put B_j(u)=J^(−u)Σ_(r in E_j)P_r r^(t+u). Then Σ_j min_(u in U_j)B_j(u)≤Σ_j B_j(u0). If one class has a strict improvement, the sum is strictly improved.

**Proof.** Each minimum is at most its value at u0. Sum these finitely many inequalities. QED.

This is an exact sufficient tail bound, not a new first-moment gain or a conclusion that a fixed-invoice κ ceiling moves. PR5775 is the common-order predecessor; the extension is the separately certified class menu.

### A03  Tilted log rank certifies a tail optimum  R and K

**Theorem.** Let B(u)=Σ_i a_i exp(u x_i) be a finite nonempty sum, with a_i>0. Then (log B)' is the probability-weighted mean of x_i under weights a_i exp(u x_i)/B(u), and (log B)'' is their variance. A zero derivative is a global minimum; a bracket with opposite derivative signs encloses a minimum.

**Proof.** Differentiate the finite positive sum and divide by B. A second differentiation gives E[x²]−E[x]²≥0. Convexity gives the supporting-line inequality, proving the stationary-point claim. Monotonicity of the derivative proves the bracket claim, allowing a flat interval. QED.

**Use and credit.** Recover unpublished Volume D, entries D038–D040, and the classical log-sum-exp identity. For A01 take x_i=log(r_i/J). This observation is already used in PR5775 and is not claimed as a fresh discovery.

### A04  A variance certificate can replace the largest-rank tariff  E and K

**Theorem.** Under a finite probability distribution let X have mean μ, variance σ² and |X−μ|≤B. For u≥0 with uB<3,

    E exp(uX) ≤ exp(uμ+u²σ²/[2(1−uB/3)]).

**Proof.** Set Y=X−μ. For every k≥2, |E Y^k|≤σ² B^(k−2). Expand the finite-support moment-generating function. Since k!≥2·3^(k−2), its terms beyond the linear term sum to at most u²σ²/[2(1−uB/3)]. Thus E exp(uY)≤1+that quantity≤its exponential. Multiply by exp(uμ). QED.

**Use.** Apply this to a paid class with probability proportional to P_r r^t and X=log(r/J). A rare high-rank class need not force every job to pay its worst log rank. All means, variances and support bounds are exact properties of the declared full class; none is inferred from a sample.

### A05  Sparse completion has an exact remainder invoice  R

**Theorem.** Suppose an actual target acts by a canonical Gaussian on K_i active axes and identity on enough declared spectator axes. To increase it to rank L, apply a canonical Gaussian on D_i=max(0,L−K_i) inactive axes of that SAME input. Split D_i=q_iJ+r_i, 0≤r_i<J. The new paid moment is Σ_i(q_iJ^t+r_i^t), omitting r_i=0 terms, and the new productive moment is Σ_i max(K_i,L)^t. If D=ΣD_i and n_a counts remainders a, the added paid moment equals

    (D−Σ_(a=1)^(J−1) a n_a)J^(t−1)+Σ_(a=1)^(J−1)n_a a^t.

**Proof.** The new Gaussian axes are disjoint from the old active axes, so their operators commute and compose to the larger canonical target. Partition those new axes into q_i blocks of J and a final block of r_i. Sum their actual ranks and moments. The displayed identity separates full-block mass from remainder mass. QED.

**Use and credit.** Recover D031–D034. Only deficient targets incur work, all old targets are debited, and no input is cloned. Actual ambient spectators, whole-job matchers and ordinary routing must be supplied; this theorem does not create them from a histogram.

## II  Whole guard portfolios and phase dependent potentials

For this section assume an explicitly supplied compiler contract uses t=1−C>0, c>1, b>0 and u>0, with guards

    F0=T_t−P_t,

    F1=c^u T_t−P_(t+u),

    F2=c^C T_t−bW.

W is the actual declared completion count, or a proved upper bound for it. These are parameterized source forms, not an assertion that every current caller uses the old c=17 or u=1/5. Strict positivity of all three is equivalent to the corresponding three normalized ratios being below one.

### A06  Guard vectors add for disjoint physical programs  E

**Theorem.** A disjoint union of actual source-compatible programs using the same c,b,u,C has guard vector equal to the sum of their guard vectors. It preserves arbitrary independent inputs and requires the sum of their owned reserves, unless a separate chronological reuse proof reduces that reserve.

**Proof.** Paid moments, productive moments and completion counts add. Each guard is linear in these three quantities. The block-diagonal physical endpoint and disjoint original banks preserve independence. QED.

**Use.** Compensate a tail-poor provider with a provider whose complete tail guard is better. Averaging reported κ values or unweighted ratios is not this construction. Source compatibility and matching common parameters are actual hypotheses.

### A07  Three source types suffice for three linear guards  E and K

**Theorem.** If a finite nonnegative real combination of guard vectors in R³ is strictly positive coordinatewise, a combination using at most three of those vectors has exactly the same positive guard vector.

**Proof.** Start with any finite representation. If more than three active vectors occur, they are linearly dependent. Choose a nonzero dependence and, after changing its sign if needed, ensure it has a positive coefficient. Subtract the largest nonnegative multiple that keeps all mixture coefficients nonnegative. At least one coefficient becomes zero; the represented vector is unchanged. Repeat. QED.

**Use.** This is a cone statement about A06's common-parameter disjoint source programs. It differs from the earlier four-type barycentric log-mixture statement for multiplicative products. It does not itself prove that the actual source menu contains a positive combination.

### A08  Strict portfolios admit integer counts with charged setup  E

**Theorem.** Suppose coefficients λ_i≥0 give Σ_i λ_i g_ij≥η>0 in every guard coordinate j. Let h_j≥0 bound one complete shared setup debit. For N>0 choose n_i=floor(Nλ_i), and let L_j=Σ_i|g_ij|. If Nη>L_j+h_j for each j, the integer program portfolio still has all guards positive after that setup.

**Proof.** Write n_i=Nλ_i+e_i with −1<e_i≤0. Then Σ_i n_i g_ij≥Nη−L_j>h_j. Subtract the complete setup debit. Every n_i is a literal nonnegative integer copy count and the physical copies use disjoint original inputs. QED.

**Use.** Turn a strict real or rational portfolio nomination into a finite fully charged construction. Count actual phase wrappers, owned storage and normalizers in h; no generic setup oracle is hidden here.

### A09  Multiplicative portfolios need a physical product contract  R

**Theorem.** Let a supplied chronological product construction have paid and target grade multisets that are actual products of its factors' grades, with common C and u, cutoffs and floors multiplied accordingly, and W_product=∏_i W_i as its actual completion count or a justified multiplicative completion upper bound. Its s-moments factor. Hence each normalized guard ratio factors, and a strict negative coordinatewise sum of log-ratio triples yields all three product contractions.

**Proof.** Expand a product of finite sums distributively: Σ_(i1,...,im)(r_i1⋯r_im)^s=∏_jΣ_i r_i^s. Apply this separately to the construction's declared paid and productive lists, then to the normalization factors. Taking logarithms turns the ratio products into coordinate sums. QED.

**Use and credit.** Recover D022–D029. A generic tensor product of Gaussian payload operators has additive active dimensions; it is NOT automatically the multiplicative-grade product assumed here. The chronological product/compiler must supply the stated grade construction, ownership and precision. The algebraic factorization alone does not supply that physical contract.

### A10  Phase dependent potentials certify nonstationary typed programs  E

**Theorem.** Let each allowed phase transition p→q carry a nonnegative child matrix A_pq. Suppose explicit positive vectors w_p and 0<θ<1 satisfy A_pq w_q≤θw_p for every allowed edge. Then every allowed length-n phase path has product sending its terminal weight vector to at most θ^n times its initial one.

**Proof.** For one edge this is the hypothesis. Nonnegative matrices preserve coordinate inequalities, so multiply the final edge inequality by the preceding matrices and apply the remaining hypotheses successively. QED.

**Use.** Match types and phases to literal source interfaces, lifetimes and branch policy. This can certify a program without a single common weight vector. PR5785 already implements the classical stationary fixed-matrix alternative; the extension is an explicit phase graph with changing weight vectors and source-checked transitions. No spectral-radius guess is needed.

## III  Joint sensitivity of the complete invoice

### A11  An entropy and curvature certificate funds a whole test  R

**Theorem.** Let

    M=Σ_k T_k k−Σ_r P_r r,

    S=Σ_r P_r r log r−Σ_k T_k k log k,

    L=Σ_r P_r r(log r)².

For every C≥0, F(C)≥M+CS−C²L/2. A positive right side therefore proves the complete first-moment test at that C.

**Proof.** F(0)=M and F'(0)=S. Also F''(x)=Σ_k T_k k^(1−x)(log k)²−Σ_r P_r r^(1−x)(log r)²≥−L for x≥0. Integrate twice. QED.

**Use and credit.** Recover the unpublished funding-certificate note and D100. Integer comparisons can certify the sign of exp(S)=∏r r^(P_r r)/∏k k^(T_k k); rational log intervals certify a quantitative S. Neither a positive relative gain nor an entropy slope alone proves a test at the desired C.

### A12  A three-guard repair box reserves every uncertain debit  E

**Theorem.** Suppose actual edits have |ΔP_r|≤a_r, |ΔT_k|≤d_k and |ΔW|≤e. At fixed C,u,c,b the maximum possible decrease of the three guards is bounded respectively by

    B0=Σ_r a_r r^t+Σ_k d_k k^t,

    B1=Σ_r a_r r^(t+u)+c^u Σ_k d_k k^t,

    B2=c^C Σ_k d_k k^t+b e.

If the old guards exceed these three quantities, every edit in that declared box preserves all guards.

**Proof.** Subtract the two exact guard expressions. Apply the triangle inequality to each signed count change and use positivity of every rank power and normalization factor. QED.

**Use.** Reserve a real allowance for uncertain restoration and target acquisition while source designers optimize them. The bounds are on actual edits of a valid program; they do not generate a program from an optimistic box.

### A13  Coalesced signed curvature is sharper than two large budgets  E

**Theorem.** Put q_r=T_r−P_r after combining equal ranks. For 0≤a≤C≤b define

    H=Σ_(q_r>0) q_r r^(1−b)(log r)²

         −Σ_(q_r<0)|q_r| r^(1−a)(log r)².

Then F''(C)≥H on [a,b]. For h≥0 with C0,C0+h in that interval,

    F(C0+h)≥F(C0)+hF'(C0)+Hh²/2.

**Proof.** Rank powers decrease with C. Bound each positive term of F'' at b and each negative term at a. The resulting lower bound is H. Integrate it with Taylor's nonnegative triangular remainder kernel. QED.

**Use.** Couple source-identity cancellation, as emphasized in PR5770, to a local rigorous κ motion. This may prove positive curvature without assuming a universal tail-dominance ordering. It is a signed sufficient bound, not a numerical root calculation.

### A14  A common parameter box preserves all three guards  E

**Theorem.** For one unchanged complete word let H_j(C,u), j=0,1,2, be its guard functions. On a rectangle around (C0,u0), assume |∂H_j/∂C|≤L_j and |∂H_j/∂u|≤K_j. Suppose H_j(C0,u0)≥m_j and an actual source edit changes H_j throughout the rectangle by at least −e_j. Every point with |C−C0|≤h, |u−u0|≤v has all changed guards positive if

    m_j>e_j+L_j h+K_j v   for j=0,1,2.

**Proof.** Move first along the C coordinate and then along u. Integrating the two derivative bounds loses at most L_jh+K_jv. Subtract the complete edit loss e_j. QED.

**Use.** Move the saving and the tail order together after a paid source change. The fixed-word condition matters: if an integer cutoff, scale or product length changes the actual invoice, freeze and rederive its own guards. PR5775's fixed-source envelope and PR5753's interval methods are predecessors; this result couples the physical edit and all guards.

### A15  Matched substitution has different updates for each guard  E

**Theorem.** Replace one outer paid job of rank R by an actual source-compatible integer scale a of an inner program with one matching productive target of rank K, where R=aK. Suppose its all-column lift multiplies every inner rank by a. Consume that one target, retain every other independent target, and add a paid fee invoice E with no new productive inputs. Then for any s>0,

    P'_s=P_out,s−R^s+a^s P_in,s+E_s,

    T'_s=T_out,s+a^s T_in,s−R^s,

    W'=W_out+W_in−1.

Consequently, with s=t+u,

    F0'=F0_out+a^t F0_in−E_t,

    F1'=F1_out+a^s[(c/a)^u T_in,t−P_in,s]

                     +R^s−c^u R^t−E_s,

    F2'=F2_out+c^C a^t T_in,t−c^C R^t−b(W_in−1).

**Proof.** Delete the outer request and the served inner target exactly once. Add the scaled inner paid/target lists and the fee list. All remaining inner originals are new independent inputs, hence the new declared completion counter is W_out+W_in−1. When the old counters are upper bounds rather than exact counts, this remains a valid upper bound for the new actual count. Substitute these three identities into each guard and collect terms. QED.

**Use and credit.** PR5754 already implements the matched first-moment substitution identity. This extension shows why copying its favorable F0 into unchanged higher/completion guards is invalid. A rank equality does not establish a whole-job match, and an invoice does not construct an all-column lift; both physical hypotheses are explicit. Productive fee jobs require their own T and W terms rather than being hidden in E.

## IV  Lifetime sensitive precision

### A16  Suffix sensitivity gives an exact deterministic error budget  R

**Theorem.** Let a finite linear program have exact maps M1,…,ML, requested output projection P, exact input and additive implementation defects δ_j. If ||δ_j||≤ε_j, then

    ||P(error at L)||≤Σ_j b_j ε_j,

    b_j=||P ML⋯M_(j+1)||.

**Proof.** Subtract the exact and implemented recurrences. Induction gives final error Σ_j ML⋯M_(j+1)δ_j. Apply P and the operator norm inequality. No independence or randomness is assumed. QED.

**Use and credit.** Recover unpublished precision Volume B, B001–B004. If one stored defect is read several times, first combine its exact suffix images before taking a norm; the same defect is not several independent adversarial errors. Integral rounding is justified only at an endpoint with a separate exact-integrality promise.

### A17  Weighted precision has an explicit optimum  R and K

**Theorem.** For a finite nonempty collection assume b_j,c_j,E>0 and the continuous model charges Σ_j c_j log2(1/ε_j), with Σ_j b_j ε_j≤E. Its minimum over positive ε_j is attained at

    ε_j*=c_j E/(b_j Csum),   Csum=Σ_j c_j.

If nonnegative bit counts are required, this statement applies when all ε_j*≤1.

**Proof.** Set y_j=b_j ε_j/E and weights λ_j=c_j/Csum. Then Σy_j≤1. Weighted AM–GM gives ∏(y_j/λ_j)^λ_j≤Σy_j≤1. Taking logs shows that Σc_j log ε_j is maximized by y_j=λ_j, proving the claimed minimum. QED.

**Use and credit.** Recover B013's heterogeneous precision allocation. Here b_j is a proved actual suffix sensitivity and c_j is a measured-by-definition per-bit scan or arithmetic coefficient. A cost nonlinear in precision needs its actual convex cost function; it cannot be replaced by a convenient linear one.

### A18  Integer precision adds less than one bit charge per site  R

**Theorem.** Under A17, choose p_j=ceil(log2(1/ε_j*)). Then Σ_j b_j 2^(−p_j)≤E and

    Σ_j c_j p_j < optimum continuous cost+Σ_j c_j,

If every optimum bit count is integral, the integer schedule attains the continuous optimum exactly.

**Proof.** Ceiling increases each bit count by less than one and decreases its associated error. Sum the cost inequalities and the error inequalities separately. QED.

**Use.** PR5794's minimum common-grid construction concerns linear parameter regions. A17–A18 instead allocate different precisions to different actual lifetimes. They are recovered ideas to instantiate on the new physical word, not a claim that PR5794 has omitted its own stated scope.

### A19  Time dependent weighted norms are an analytic gauge  E

**Theorem.** For positive coordinate weights d, define ||x||_d=max_i |x_i|/d_i. If |M|d≤βe coordinatewise for positive output weights e, then ||Mx||_e≤β||x||_d. For a chronology M_j with |M_j|d_(j−1)≤β_jd_j, every suffix has the product of its β_j bounds between its declared weighted spaces.

**Proof.** If ||x||_d≤z, then |x|≤zd, so |Mx|≤|M||x|≤zβe. This proves one step. Compose these inequalities for the suffix. QED.

**Use.** Choose analysis weights separately at each lifetime cut to avoid one worst-coordinate norm everywhere. These are norms used in the proof, not executable free physical rescalings. The final output conversion, actual query normalization and coefficient height remain charged. Local weighted-norm certificates and the project block-energy bounds are precedents.

### A20  A forced phase potential controls all rounding accumulation  E

**Theorem.** Let every legal phase transition p→q have an entrywise nonnegative matrix B_pq and a nonnegative forcing vector δ_pq, propagating nonnegative error bounds by e_next≤B_pq e_current+δ_pq. Suppose positive weights w_p satisfy B_pq w_p≤θw_q and δ_pq≤ηw_q for all legal transitions, with 0≤θ<1 and η≥0. If e0≤z0w_initial, then after n transitions

    en≤[θ^n z0+η(1−θ^n)/(1−θ)]w_terminal.

**Proof.** A bound e_current≤zw_p gives e_next≤(θz+η)w_q. Iterate this scalar inequality and sum the finite geometric series. QED.

**Use.** Combine the explicit source phase graph of A10 with an error forcing vector instead of treating each local precision defect as though it lived for the longest possible word. PR5785 handles a fixed nonnegative matrix; phase-dependent maps and changing error norms require these additional source contracts. A noncontracting overestimate is not a disproof of the real program.

## V  Word RAM escape routes with explicit prices

### A21  Guarded lanes execute bounded integer linear blocks exactly  E

**Theorem.** Suppose a scalar straight-line block uses componentwise addition, multiplication by declared fixed nonnegative integers, and bank swaps on k nonnegative lanes. Every intermediate lane is below 2^q. Choose integer lane width b≥max(1,q) and a machine word width w≥kb. Packing a=(a_0,…,a_(k−1)) as Σ_i a_i2^(bi) makes each block instruction the corresponding whole-word instruction, without cross-lane carries or word overflow.

**Proof.** At every step the true per-lane result is less than the radix 2^b. Therefore its positional-radix sum has precisely those digits and no carry into another lane. Its packed value is less than 2^(kb)≤2^w. Induct over the block. QED.

**Use.** Signed or complex values can use separate positive/negative and real/imaginary banks, but q must bound those actual banks, not just their cancelling difference. Rational per-lane division and arbitrary permutations are not covered. Input packing, output extraction, phase boundaries and every address operation are additional work.

### A22  Segmented packing retains a literal movement bill  E

**Theorem.** Suppose a uniform word-RAM realization has n lanes of width b≤w, L lane-wise operations covered by A21, and m permutation boundaries. Put k=floor(w/b). If all permutation tables and their construction together cost Q(n), the packed realization costs

    O(L ceil(n/k)+m n+n+Q(n))

word operations, provided every actual address and value fits w bits and the instruction set includes the stated masks, shifts or exact equivalent operations.

**Proof.** Each lane-wise instruction operates once on each of ceil(n/k) packed words. At a permutation boundary, extract each lane with a paid mask/shift, scatter it according to the paid table and insert it into an initialized destination word; this is O(n). Input packing and final unpacking cost O(n). Include Q(n). QED.

**Use.** Apply this to maximal valid linear blocks of the actual source word. It gives no speedup if b is already Θ(w), and it never makes a native permutation free. An address descriptor requiring more than constant work per lane must replace the displayed m n term by its actual construction/execution cost.

### A23  Operation classes give a sharper exact bit bill  E and R

**Theorem.** In the declared Problem 1.8 logarithmic-cost RAM model, let an actual halting trace on w-bit words have Q executed multiplication/division/left-shift steps, L other linear-cost data instructions and J control or past-end fetch steps. Its bit charge is exactly Qw²+Lw+J, hence is at most (Q+L+J)(w+1)².

**Proof.** This is the sum of the source model's per-instruction charges: quadratic, linear or one. Partition the actual executed trace by those three classes and sum. The final bound follows termwise. QED.

**Use and credit.** `Mathproof.Meta.Problem18Lax13BitCost20261007` and `Problem18RAMRootBitCost20261007` provide the explicit cost-model precedent, not a fast integer-multiplication theorem. An operation reduction in unit word steps does not automatically preserve an improvement in bit cost. Count its expensive operations and packing/unpacking as well. No TM simulation is required to state or use this separate RAM bit charge.

### A24  Positive precision and resource constraints form convex subproblems  E and K

**Theorem.** Let positive resource variables z_i obey constraints of the form

    Σ_l a_jl ∏_i z_i^(b_jli)≤1,   a_jl>0.

In logarithmic coordinates x_i=log z_i, every such constraint defines a convex feasible set. Any intersection of these constraints and affine bounds in x is convex.

**Proof.** The left side becomes Σ_l a_jl exp(Σ_i b_jli x_i), a positive sum of exponentials of affine functions. Its Hessian is a sum of nonnegative multiples of outer products, hence is positive semidefinite. Its sublevel set and an intersection of convex sets are convex. QED.

**Use.** Source-derived rounding, magnitude and resource inequalities sometimes have this positive form after a frozen physical word and a fixed saving are chosen. This extends the linear parameter regions addressed by PR5792/PR5794 to a stated nonlinear subclass. A signed target-minus-paid moment or a ratio of two positive sums is not automatically such a constraint; the whole κ problem has not been declared convex.

### A25  Strict nonlinear source witnesses admit dyadic parameters  E and K

**Theorem.** Let a real vector z* lie strictly inside a finite positive box and satisfy finitely many C1 inequalities h_j(z*)≤−η_j<0. Suppose |∂h_j/∂z_i|≤M_ji on that box. A sufficiently fine coordinatewise dyadic rounding z of z* remains in the box and satisfies all inequalities whenever

    2^(−p) Σ_i M_ji < η_j

for every j and its rounding error is at most 2^(−p) per coordinate.

**Proof.** Choose the rounding fine enough to stay inside the positive distance from z* to the box boundary. Integrate the coordinate derivatives along the segment from z* to z. The total increase of h_j is at most 2^(−p)Σ_iM_ji<η_j. QED.

**Use.** Convert an interior real witness of A24's source-derived resource model into actual dyadic parameters. Exact equalities must first be parameterized in a grid-compatible way: x=1/3 is not made dyadic by density. PR5794's exact minimum-grid certificate is a predecessor for linear constraints; this sufficient nonlinear margin theorem supplies a new interface for source-specific extensions, without asserting minimum precision.

## Five composed routes

### Route A  Source-class adaptive completion

**Construction.** A01–A05 give origin-specific tail orders and exact deficient-target tariffs. Freeze the physical caller and its class partition; improve the analysis of its actual complete invoice.

**Deliverable for another contributor.** A source-bound class table, exact deficit/remainder account, rational order brackets, and strict normalized tail inequalities for the same word. Compare against PR5775's fixed-source optimum rather than against a stale offset.

**When it matters.** It can remove a completion bottleneck on a structurally improved invoice. A first moment that already fails at a target saving cannot be rescued by calling the same invoice's tail estimate better.

### Route B  Sparse heterogeneous complete suppliers

**Construction.** Normalize actual compatible suppliers to common guards, find a positive guard-cone combination, reduce its support by A07 and integerize with A08. Use A09 only where the separate chronological product contract really exists.

**Deliverable.** Explicit copy counts, disjoint original banks, common-interface normalizers, full P,T,W and all finite shared setup costs. An exact positive guard vector is the useful finite certificate.

**When it matters.** Different physical mechanisms may have complementary first/higher/completion margins. This route exchanges those margins through a real complete batch rather than chasing another tiny change to one fixed invoice.

### Route C  Lifetime typed recursion and precision

**Construction.** Refine the source by actual helper lifetimes and phase policy. Use A10 for child costs, A19–A20 for error transport and A17–A18 for per-site precision. Couple the result with the geometry/scheduling volume's explicit source changes.

**Deliverable.** A finite phase graph with source transitions, exact nonnegative matrices, rational positive vectors, a deterministic error-forcing bound and an integer precision/storage prescription.

**When it matters.** It can admit a schedule whose cost or numerical safety is lost in a single worst-case aggregate. A different vector for each phase is useful only if every permitted source transition has been covered.

### Route D  Packed scalar blocks on an actual Word RAM

**Construction.** Extract A21-compatible integer linear blocks, retain all movements and permutations by A22, and price the actual trace by A23. Use the explicit Problem 1.8 RAM program/word-bound precedents as machine-model infrastructure, not as a near-linear multiplication bound.

**Deliverable.** A finite program with input/output codecs, actual standard-word fit and address bounds, magnitude/precision guards, movement tables or their construction algorithm, and both word and logarithmic-bit cost expressions.

**When it matters.** A growing lane factor with controlled movement can change the architecture's asymptotics. Report its model and complexity directly before comparing it with the selected TM κ; equal numerical labels across models do not establish equal runtimes.

### Route E  Guard-valued source composition

**Construction.** Combine structurally legal changes through A15, reserve uncertain costs with A12, certify sensitivity with A11–A14, then solve any positive resource subsystem by A24–A25. Preserve current PR attribution and exact source types throughout.

**Deliverable.** An acyclic source derivation with one-use matched targets, original-field identifiers, complete guard updates, fresh precision/owned costs and strict rational acceptance inequalities. A winning candidate need not come with a proof that the search was optimal.

**When it matters.** This is an interface for contributors to combine many local ideas into one actual caller. The theorem can succeed only on the same unchanged source word that its certificate describes.
