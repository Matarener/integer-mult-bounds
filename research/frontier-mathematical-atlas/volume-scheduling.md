# Temporal helper spaces, query sharing and causal delivery

Dugongue, with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance. The results below are informal sufficient constructions. No solver, mathematical computation, source replay or Lean check was run. They do not provide a new complete multiplication exponent.

The selected revision-8 result is conditional κ = 0.00310045 from PR5599; the five framework assumptions remain. Larger pending values belong to their exact PRs, not to the selected record. This volume addresses chronology and physical independence, where a static span calculation alone does not supply an algorithm.

## Source scope and notation

The frontier baseline is canonical main `de5ec2e7a6074068452f45b1a191b5c72832df4a`. The mathematical source inspection included the selected helper-subspace proof, the actual paid-meeting proof of [PR5751](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5751), the asynchronous proof of [PR5786](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5786), and the independent first-hub audit [PR5800](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5800). Their scalar/native/physical/compiler scopes differ and are retained here. In particular, the source prefix can be singular; none of the following constructions assumes its inverse exists.

All vector spaces are over a declared field, and all matrices act on the indicated field indices. Payload maps may be substituted only when their actual ordinary or Gaussian implementations are supplied. A matrix on owner indices is ordinary only at a proved common physical chart and with admitted scalar coefficients. Every basis change, copy, route, lifetime, query subdivision and owned CLEAR has positive cost. Original inputs are arbitrary and independent. Owned buffers are scrubbed at entry, never productive, and cleared at exit.

A result marked **recovered** is curated from the unpublished scheduling atlas or prior deferred-binding notes. **Extension** means a new combined application relative to the inspected source scope, not a claim that its classical linear algebra is new. **Framework** records a standard sufficient construction needed by later entries. Proposed source instantiations remain questions until their actual maps, chronology and complete bill are supplied.

## Route A Singular-prefix-safe cut constructions

### S01 Exact splice with a supplied cut map

**Framework theorem.** Let P be an old prefix and S its suffix. Suppose an explicitly implemented new prefix P' and an explicitly implemented map R obey R P'=P J on the full desired input space, and use suffix S R. Then the new complete endpoint is S P J. No injectivity of P is required.

**Proof.** Associate the products: (S R)P'=S(RP')=S P J. The identity must hold on every original and dirty owned input column. R is an actual program with its own price; it is not an unexecuted inverse of P. If J discards old independent inputs their productive targets are debited. If J introduces new independent inputs, their actual boundary and output contracts must be proved. ∎

**Use.** Construct changed cut interfaces by a forward identity rather than searching for an inverse of a prefix made singular by INIT/CLEAR. This is compatible with the stronger source checks emphasized in PR5771.

### S02 Reconstruct only the observable needed after a cut

**Framework theorem.** For linear maps P:X→W and T:X→Y on finite-dimensional spaces, the condition ker P ⊆ ker T supplies a linear L:W→Y with L P=T. Given bases, L is constructed by assigning L(Px)=Tx on im P and extending over a chosen complement.

**Proof.** If Px=Px', then x−x' belongs to ker P and therefore T(x−x')=0; the assignment is well-defined and linear on im P. A basis of im P extends to W, and values on the added basis vectors may be set to zero. This constructs L and proves L P=T. ∎

**Use.** Restore a particular helper observable from the real cut state without pretending to restore every old input. Physical delivery of L must still be ordinary at the actual chart or be explicitly priced. This is a positive quotient-factorization theorem, not a claim that a rank test supplies its physical implementation.

### S03 Block deferred binding before any outgoing read

**Recovered theorem.** Let a block h of old helper entrances have no outgoing read before a cut. Suppose its old private prefix is Lh+b with L explicitly invertible as a mathematical map. The old retained endpoint is independent of h. Replace that prefix by an owned block and at the cut write any causally available, physically admitted value v. Retaining the suffix preserves the retained endpoint and debits every retired helper target.

**Proof.** Before the cut, all other fields are independent of h. The counterfactual old entrance L^(-1)(v−b) makes the old cut state equal the new one. The old retained endpoint was independent of that entrance, so it remains unchanged. This inverse is a correctness parameter, not a runtime operation. The actual program computes v from its available operands, pays initialization/binding/cleanup and uses no unobserved future input. ∎

**Use.** Retire blocks rather than single helpers when an actual block first-read window exists. The source-bound nine-helper BIT construction is a predecessor; this statement does not transfer those cuts to the current P104 word.

### S04 Zero an entire first-read receiver block

**Extension theorem.** In S03, suppose the first outgoing block read is Y←Y+B h, where B and B^(-1) have explicit admitted ordinary implementations at the actual common chart. Bind h:=−B^(-1)Y immediately before that read. The entire Y block becomes zero, so any next linear query acting only on Y can be omitted on the known whole-zero input.

**Proof.** The retained read computes Y+B(−B^(-1)Y)=0. S03 preserves the eventual retained endpoint. The omitted query would return zero on every address and column. Acquire its required zero output header and retain the suffix. Receiver information moved into the newly owned helper block rather than disappearing. ∎

**Bill.** Remove only literal omitted queries and old unobserved-prefix queries. Debit all old helper targets. Add every binding, header, buffer, lifetime and restoration cost. A data-coordinate zero does not establish whole-payload zero.

### S05 Schur-complement block zeroing with explicit scalar programs

**Extension theorem.** At one dormant-block cut let two receiver blocks have values

    q1=Y1+A h1+B h2,   q2=Y2+C h1+D h2.

Suppose D and E=A−B D^(-1) C have explicitly supplied ordinary inverse programs at that cut. Bind

    h1=−E^(-1)(Y1−B D^(-1)Y2),
    h2=−D^(-1)(Y2+C h1).

Then q1=q2=0, with the retained endpoint preserved by S03.

**Proof.** Substitution gives q2=0. Substituting h2 into q1 leaves Y1−BD^(-1)Y2+E h1, which is zero by the chosen h1. The order shown is causal because Y1,Y2 are current values and h2 uses the just-computed h1. No inverse of the full source prefix is taken. ∎

**Use.** This is a direct scheduling application of the Schur block-system elimination already present in the local proof tree. It offers a concrete way to search coupled zeroing cuts; it does not assert that a required inverse is ordinary merely because its scalar matrix is nonsingular.

### S06 Several deferred bindings compose in first-read order

**Recovered theorem.** Apply S03 at cuts ordered by first outgoing read. A later unbound helper has no influence on an earlier binding operand, provided that operand is built only from current retained fields and already bound helpers. The bindings can therefore be chosen successively without solving a simultaneous fixed-point equation.

**Proof.** An influence on an earlier binding value would require an earlier outgoing read of the supposedly later helper. This contradicts its cut definition. Choose each counterfactual old entrance after fixing earlier choices; it changes none of the already matched retained prefix values. Induction establishes a matching old execution at every cut and hence the old retained endpoint. ∎

**Use.** Dependencies from an earlier bound helper into a later binding are permitted. Dependencies from an unbound future helper are not. All source versions, matching maps and source-head identities remain explicit.

## Route B Many-channel asynchronous hubs

### S07 An explicit block boundary basis from unit pivots

**Extension theorem.** Let C be an r-by-n collection matrix and D an n-by-r delivery matrix on an identity helper block whose members share the same entrance and exit charts, with C D=I_r. Choose r pivot coordinates I and write the other coordinates as J. If D_I=I_r, the two ordinary block additions

    pivot -= C_J outside,
    outside += D_J pivot

give an invertible Q satisfying Q E_I=D and C Q=E_I^T. Their literal reverse order and opposite signs give Q^(-1).

**Proof.** In I,J order the product is

    Q=[[I,−C_J],[D_J,I−D_J C_J]].

Its pivot columns equal D. Also C_I+C_JD_J=I by C D=I. Multiplying C Q gives [I,0]. Each factor is a triangular block ADD with identity diagonal, so invertibility needs no additional scalar division. ∎

**Use.** Normalize many channels simultaneously without treating a dense inverse as free. A nonidentity pivot block may be used only with its own explicitly admitted inverse program. The rank-one unit-pivot construction in PR5786/5800 is a direct predecessor.

### S08 Simultaneous independent-target splitting with the stronger cut identities

**Extension theorem.** Let P,S be an actual cut and suffix, T=S P, and let T be identity on a helper coordinate block G with no cross terms to the other original outputs. Suppose r-row A and r-column B use only fields actually at one intermediate chart F. Supply C,D on G with

    A P=C,  S B=D,  A B=I_r,  C D=I_r,  P D=B.

Supply ordinary common-chart bases Q,J with Q E_I=D, C Q=E_I^T, J B=E_I and E_I^T J=A. Assume all of G has common entrance chart U and exit chart V. Then r old helper targets U→V can be replaced by independent targets U→F and F→V, with r genuinely new arbitrary public inputs.

**Proof.** At the transformed cut the pivot rows of J P Q are E_I^T, and its pivot columns are E_I. Thus those r cut fields contain exactly the r old independent pivot inputs and no other cut field depends on them. Retire those fields at F; substitute r new independent inputs z into the same cut positions. After J^(-1), the suffix correction is D(z−x_I). Since Q is supported on the identity helper block, Q commutes with T. The final Q^(-1) therefore gives T x+E_I(z−x_I), while the retired fields already hold x_I. Every other output, including owned zero outputs, is retained. ∎

**Use.** The extra identity P D=B is the constructive isolation condition used in the paid-meeting proof. It must not be omitted because two short scalar pairings happen to be one. All chart/basis/native costs and independent-target matchers remain charged.

### S09 A rank-r asynchronous hub with retained difference buffers

**Extension theorem.** Let the source endpoint be identity on a helper block G with common entrance U and exit V. At one actual pivot cut F supply complete collection rows C from contacts no later than the cut and complete injection columns D from contacts no earlier than it. Require C D=I_r, D_I=I_r and support only on G, including zero dirty-owned dependence and zero correction on owned outputs. All collection and injection operations use their actual F presentations. Then the rank-r version of the asynchronous exchange splits the r pivot helper targets.

**Construction and proof.** Apply Q from S07 at the common entrance. Initialize r owned buffers and collect C Q x=x_I. Write y for the current pivot vector; it need not equal x_I. Introduce fresh arbitrary input vector z at F and perform

    swap(pivot,buffer); swap(fresh,buffer); buffer -= pivot.

The three vectors become pivot=x_I, fresh=y, buffer=z−x_I. Retire the old pivots and rename every old suffix reference to their fresh replacements, including final basis references. The old continuation starts with its exact old value y. The later contacts add D(z−x_I) to the full source output. Applying terminal Q^(-1) yields T x+E_I(z−x_I), exactly as in S08. The buffers still contain z−x_I and require the charged CLEAR after their last injection. ∎

**Use.** This extends the existing rank-one asynchronous mechanism to explicit many-channel collection/delivery matrices. It requires neither a simultaneous collection meeting nor a prefix inverse, but it does require a real common pivot cut and all advertised temporal contacts. Rank grades follow only from actual nested charts and native matching.

### S10 Causal triangular Gram correction

**Extension theorem.** Suppose a proposed collection C0 and delivery D have G=C0 D unit lower triangular in channel order. At a common collection chart set C=G^(-1)C0. If C0's channel i is available by time i and every earlier channel buffer stays available, C can be collected causally using only current and earlier buffers.

**Proof.** The inverse of I+N is I−N+N^2−...+(-N)^(r−1) because strictly lower triangular N satisfies N^r=0. It is lower triangular with unit diagonal and C D=I. Its row i uses no buffer later than i. Implement its finite triangular row operations with admitted scalar coefficients, then apply S07/S09 if the pivot and delivery contracts hold. ∎

**Use.** Search for triangular rather than diagonal collection/delivery pairings. This relaxes an orthogonality restriction without importing future values. Cross-chart buffer mixing needs paid matching maps; triangularity alone does not make those maps ordinary.

### S11 Repeated splits along a genuine chart chain

**Extension theorem.** Let U=F0 strictly inside F1 strictly inside ... strictly inside Fm=V. If each next split is proved by S08 or S09 on the EXACT word produced by its predecessor, one original U→V helper trajectory is replaced by m independent targets of grades a_i=dim(Fi)−dim(F(i−1)). Its productive moment change is

    sum_i a_i^t − (sum_i a_i)^t >0  for 0<t<1 and m>1.

**Proof.** Apply each all-input split theorem to its current continuation input. Each step introduces a new arbitrary input and retires an independent earlier one, preserving every other endpoint. The ranks telescope along the strict chain. Strict subadditivity of x^t gives the displayed target gain. ∎

**Use.** This is a route to many independent targets, not a license to reuse old event indices after a split. Add every access subdivision, buffer, boundary basis, target normalizer and completion-field increase. The full signed gain can still be negative after those charges.

## Route C Causal query bases and changed cache delivery

The next results concern observational query requests: an actual current input version is preserved and its answer is placed in an owned output consumed by later code. They do not silently replace an in-place native MOVE by overwriting an independent field. All query inputs and cached answers use their declared common physical ABIs, and every scalar coefficient used below has an admitted ordinary recipe.

### S12 A causal query basis needs no future acquisition

**Extension theorem.** Fix one linear query Q of grade q. At times i=1,...,m its actual requested inputs are v_i=ell_i(u), where the ell_i are scalar rows on one underlying original-input vector u. At time i the value v_i is actually available. Preserve cached answers at their common output ABI. Query v_i only when ell_i is outside the span of previously acquired rows; otherwise reconstruct Qv_i from their cached answers. This uses exactly rank{ell_1,...,ell_m} Q requests.

**Proof.** Inductively the acquired rows are independent and span every processed row. A dependent row has an explicit linear expression in them; linearity of Q gives the same expression in their answers. An independent row is queried at its actual time, never earlier, and enlarges the span by one. Its answer is copied to every old owned consumer output with its literal ordinary operations. Thus all old consumers see their exact old operands, while the number of acquisitions equals the final rank. ∎

**Use.** This adds chronology and complete reconstruction to the static acquisition-basis problem in PR5791. With equal query grade, it removes m−rank requests; source preservation, cache lifetimes, reconstruction, routing and erasure are separately charged. Different source versions may participate only through a proved all-input row relation.

### S13 Compress a cache to its future demand intersection

**Extension theorem.** At a cut let V be the span of acquired query rows and W the span of all remaining request rows for the same Q. The cache may be replaced by answers for a basis of U=V∩W. Future causal basis acquisition then uses exactly dim W−dim U new Q requests, the same count achievable from the whole old cache V by the S12 rule.

**Proof.** Each basis row of U is a linear combination of the old cached rows, so its answer is reconstructed ordinarily before clearing discarded owned cache slots. Starting with U and processing the actual future requests by S12 ends with span W and makes dim W−dim U rank increases. Starting with V instead ends with V+W and makes dim(V+W)−dim V=dim W−dim(V∩W) increases. The counts agree. ∎

**Use.** Cache fewer linear combinations rather than merely assigning old fixed lifetimes to fewer slots. This changes the values being retained, a degree of freedom not supplied by a fixed-lifetime allocator. The theorem optimizes acquisition count within this common-Q scalar-row model; weighted costs and different frames need a fresh argument.

### S14 Commutant-valued reconstruction

**Extension theorem.** Suppose Q is linear and explicit ordinary maps A_j satisfy Q A_j=A_j Q. If a requested input v equals sum_j A_j u_j as an actual operator identity, then its answer can be assembled as sum_j A_j(Q u_j) from existing same-version cached answers, with no new Q request.

**Proof.** Distribute Q over the sum and use each displayed commutation identity. Execute the supplied A_j words on owned copies and add their results into the owned consumer output. Every original input and cached donor is retained; all scratch is initialized and cleared. ∎

**Use.** This is a concrete noncanonical matrix-valued cache contract beyond scalar canonical delivery. The coefficient algebra need not be a field, so the static greedy matroid-basis argument is not automatically valid. Actual ordinary A_j and their phase-correct commutation identities are the witness, not an optimistic information span.

### S15 Several query operators can share one exact right factor

**Framework theorem.** Suppose actual requests have Q_i=L_i Q0 R on the same immutable physical input version x, with explicitly ordinary R,L_i and a supplied exact query implementation Q0. Compute Q0(Rx) once in owned cache and distribute L_i times that answer to all owned outputs. The exact Gaussian bill is that of the single Q0, plus any explicitly declared nonordinary connector words.

**Proof.** Each distributed output is L_i Q0 R x=Q_i x. Preserve x and the cached answer with owned copies; the old consumers are unchanged. All input preparation, ordinary outputs, copies and cleanup are finite priced work. No cached copy is an independent productive target. ∎

**Use.** This is useful when the query family has one genuinely shared right factor. Coincident frame dimensions or a common bank name do not prove that factorization. The scoped separate-delivery and one-frame exclusions of PR5765 remain valid for their exact old contracts.

### S16 Move a cached answer through an ordinary intertwiner

**Extension theorem.** Let source version x change to Gx by an old instruction. If an explicitly supplied ordinary L obeys Q G=L Q on all inputs, update cached z=Qx by z←Lz. The cache is now the correct answer for the changed source version without a new Q request.

**Proof.** The new cache is LQx=QGx. The source's original G instruction remains and is paid; the cache update uses its actual L normalizer and finite cost. Repeated updates compose in chronological order, so later consumers receive the answer for their exact versions. ∎

**Use.** This relaxes immutable-version reuse to a proved transport orbit. The formal expression QGQ^(-1) is not a free ordinary map: L must actually be supplied, including phase. This is a precise changed contract for investigations extending retained native copies.

### S17 A fully paid multilevel gather tree

**Extension theorem.** Give a rooted tree actual leaf values x_j and one explicitly implemented linear map A_e on every child-to-parent edge. At each internal node sum the transformed child values. The root equals

    sum_j (ordered product of A_e along leaf j's path) x_j.

Each edge word is executed once. Its total paid Gaussian inventory is the sum of the edge-word inventories, and no gathered owned value is productive.

**Proof.** Induct from leaves to root using linearity at each sum. Each edge sees one already computed child value and is used exactly once. Assign owned scratch to internal nodes and keep a node until all parent consumers finish; clear it afterward. This gives a causal literal program and the stated complete edge bill. ∎

**Use.** A proposed replacement for an old query must prove equality of the root operator to that query on every original/helper input, then price every ingress and final delivery. The tree can use new intermediate frames and matrix-valued maps, a scope expressly distinct from PR5765's one-final-gather model. A bare tensor-span witness is not this tree.

### S18 Shared dirty preparation and fanout

**Recovered theorem.** Let A:X→R and B_j:R→Z_j be homomorphisms, with supplied signed shear implementations. On arbitrary dirty r, perform r+=A(x), every z_j+=B_j(r), r−=A(x), then every z_j−=B_j(r). The result is z_j+=B_j A(x), with r restored. Its cost is two preparations plus two executions of each consumer.

**Proof.** Each target receives B_j(r0+A(x))−B_j(r0)=B_jA(x). Distinct targets and preserved x prevent interference. The dirty offset cancels; it was never assumed zero. ∎

**Use.** Reuse one dirty preparation across many consumers, saving repeated preparation and lifetimes when its complete two-sided cost beats separate words. This is classical commutator algebra recovered from unpublished atlas entry C003, not a new elementary identity or a free Gaussian inverse.

## Route D Chronology and physical stock

### S19 Move a last consumer earlier through actual commuting work

**Recovered theorem.** An actual consumer may be moved earlier through instructions whose data/control reads and writes are disjoint from its writes and whose writes are disjoint from its reads; common immutable reads are permitted. If it becomes a cache value's last use, that value's lifetime shortens and the peak number of live cache fields cannot increase.

**Proof.** The stated conditions let adjacent instructions commute on all states and preserve control. Repeated adjacent swaps move the consumer without changing outputs. Only the cache's unused suffix is deleted from its lifetime; every time-slice live set loses zero or one field and never gains one. ∎

**Use.** Retiming is a physical trace claim, not an appeal to a static dataflow rank. It can create a common contact or reduce owned stock while leaving the Gaussian multiset fixed. More scalar savings alone are not a κ improvement. This extends unpublished atlas entry C017.

### S20 Restored intervals admit peak-stock allocation

**Recovered theorem.** For fixed half-open lifetimes of unit-sized owned fields with identical ABI and entry/exit ownership contract, peak overlap K suffices for allocation into K slots without moving a live value. If K borrowed dirty slots are all available throughout the allocation horizon and every use restores its slot, the same statement holds for those slots under their owner-return contract.

**Proof.** At a birth, at most K−1 other lifetimes are live, so some slot is free. Reuse it only after its previous right endpoint and restoration. Induction over births proves collision freedom; the same boundary restoration establishes correctness for dirty borrowing. A simultaneous live set of size K proves optimality in this fixed model. ∎

**Use.** The old atlas already contained this interval argument; weighted freely assignable pools are now implemented in PR5763. The new useful task is to change a semantic lifetime through S13 or S19, then give the allocator its justified new intervals. Do not claim the allocation tool itself as new.

### S21 Phase composition shares a restored scratch bank

**Recovered theorem.** Suppose each phase uses one common compatible temporary ABI, has a complete functional contract and returns every temporary to its declared ownership state. Phases with temporary demands K_i may use one bank of max_i K_i slots, plus all explicitly boundary-crossing values.

**Proof.** At each phase boundary the temporary bank satisfies exactly the next phase's entry precondition. Rename that phase's private slots into the shared bank and compose its functional contract. Values crossing the boundary are excluded from the bank until their own lifetimes end. ∎

**Use.** This is a sufficient route from local hub lifetimes to one caller stock bound. It preserves suspended-instance reserves and has no automatic Gaussian-call saving. Reversible-pebble planning such as PR5780 supplies a separate fixed-DAG search, not the missing dirty-owner contract.

### S22 Recompute across a genuinely unused gap

**Recovered theorem.** Let an owned value v be produced from immutable retained inputs, used in two clusters and unused in the intervening gap. If an explicit producer can be rerun before cluster two, release v after cluster one and rerun the producer. This removes v from the gap, at the cost of the extra producer, all input retention and every changed acquisition/clear/access step.

**Proof.** The immutable operands make both productions identical. There is no consumer in the removed gap, and each later consumer sees the same value. Owned release is legal only under its actual overwrite/erasure contract. Count the repeated producer and changed lifetimes literally. ∎

**Use.** Combine source-preserving caches with recomputation rather than treating a fixed pure dependency DAG as the whole physical scheduling problem. The classical pebbling idea and unpublished atlas C018 are predecessors; changed native costs are the actual research question.

## Route E Mixing and composing complete improvements

### S23 Rational mixtures of actual programs are constructible

**Framework theorem.** Let finitely many actual finite converters have compatible declared parent/input/output semantics for an explicitly allowed direct-sum assembly. For nonnegative rational weights lambda_i with sum one, choose a positive integer q with q lambda_i integral. Running q lambda_i independent copies realizes total inventories

    P=sum_i q lambda_i P_i,  T=sum_i q lambda_i T_i,
    W=sum_i q lambda_i W_i,

plus every assembly-specific connector cost.

**Proof.** Allocate disjoint original input banks and each converter's own owned reserves. Run the finite programs sequentially and retain all outputs. Independence and their functional contracts give the direct sum. Count each query, target and completion job once. q is fixed before the growing payload size, so the assembly is a finite explicit word. ∎

**Use.** Mix constructions instead of forcing all local calls to use one mechanism, but only through a proved compatible assembly. A fractional LP mixture does not itself prove that a particular fixed caller can realize those weights; any connectors and extra targets are part of the bill.

### S24 Strict feasible mixtures have rational witnesses

**Framework theorem.** For finitely many compatible programs at fixed tested exponent and fixed compiler parameters, suppose their normalized first-moment, higher-moment and completion guards become strict linear inequalities in their mixture weights after multiplying positive denominators. If a real weight vector satisfies them strictly, a rational weight vector does too and is realizable by S23 whenever its direct-sum assembly is admitted.

**Proof.** There are finitely many continuous linear functions with positive margins at the real point. A sufficiently small neighborhood preserves all margins and nonnegativity within its supporting face. Rational points are dense in that finite simplex face. Choose a rational point there, clear denominators, and apply S23. ∎

**Use.** Converts an actual compatible convex portfolio into a finite witness rather than a limiting oracle. Feasibility alone is not a selected-frontier update; source semantics, completion count and finite ordinary bounds must still be instantiated. Exact LP tools such as PR5767 are prior infrastructure.

### S25 Compatible local rewrites compose with their full signed bills

**Framework theorem.** Suppose each rewrite has a proved actual entry/exit interface, exact source-version bindings and owned restoration. Suppose its modified read/write/lifetime region is disjoint from every other rewrite's modified region, except for shared immutable donors; declared surrounding main interfaces are unchanged. Then the rewrites compose, and their complete paid/target changes add.

**Proof.** A rewrite sees the same entry data as in its proof because other rewrites preserve its donors and private region. Its full postcondition establishes the old surrounding interface and its own explicit changed helper targets. Apply the rewrites in chronological order; source-version and ownership invariants are preserved at every boundary. Disjoint query/target record identities make the invoice changes a disjoint sum. ∎

**Use.** This supplies a sufficient semantic contract for edit-selection tools such as PR5759. A graph edge saying two candidate edits look compatible is not the contract itself. When edits share a modified source version or target, construct a new joint word rather than add their advertised gains.

## Open next questions

The following are finite research targets, not claims that their underlying classical mathematics is new. For every candidate, the success artifact is an exact physical word, independent-target matchers, chronology/owned witness, complete invoice and strict current compiler margins.

### SQ01 Many-channel asynchronous hubs

Can the current P104 source supply a rank-r collection/delivery pair of S09 with r substantially larger than one, sharing its access subdivisions and buffer lifetime? First construct exact C,D on one common intermediate frame and check C D=I_r with unit pivot block. Compare paid access plus all new target grades against the actual predecessor. This extends PR5786 rather than duplicating its three sequential hubs.

### SQ02 Triangular temporal pairings

Can a legal collection schedule yield unit lower-triangular C0 D where a diagonal-pairing search fails, with all row corrections causally available at a common chart? A concrete first step is one two- or three-channel triangular witness and its literal correction word. Charge every connector and test all dirty columns.

### SQ03 New rank from genuinely different contact geometry

Can a new owned-memory route or contact outside the exact PR5766 three-meeting model produce the missing second independent delivery direction for a blocked helper? The changed contract must be explicit before a coefficient search. Longer words inside those same excluded meetings are not a new route.

### SQ04 Shared causal query bases

Does an actual family of observational retained-copy requests admit a scalar common-Q row relation that removes at least one acquisition under S12? Bind immutable source versions and all physical ABIs first. Static span redundancy without causal reconstruction does not answer the question.

### SQ05 Future-demand cache compression

For a useful current source region, is dim(V intersect W) much smaller than the number of retained query answers, and can the basis conversion be emitted with permitted coefficients? Supply one phase boundary and its exact owned-cache replacement. Quantify stock, route and reconstruction work before claiming a compiler benefit.

### SQ06 Ordinary intertwiner transport

Which actual native updates G in the retained-copy source have phase-correct ordinary L with QG=LQ for a later required Q? Start with one actual pair and its literal normalizer. This changes the immutable-source contract and may permit reuse across mutations that currently force reacquisition.

### SQ07 A multilevel noncanonical gather

Can a source-bound gather tree with explicit noncanonical edge maps beat the tested centre charge once every edge, final delivery and buffer is included? This is outside PR5765's stated one-frame canonical delivery scope. Require operator equality on fresh helper namespaces, not merely a relaxed information-span certificate.

### SQ08 Block Schur zeroing

Is there an uncovered dormant first-read block whose ordinary receiver-coupling Schur complement is explicitly invertible with admitted scalar operations, so S05 omits a whole group of queries? The first artifact should be the exact block matrices, current receiver charts and fully debited helper targets, before any κ pricing.

### SQ09 Source-aware mixing

Can compatible variants with different target distributions be mixed in the actual caller to improve all current normalized guards, with exact rational multiplicities and fully paid connectors? Check the caller's true finite stage counts and outer rows; an abstract convex average alone does not instantiate its topology.

### SQ10 Combined target growth and live stock

Can an S09/S11 construction add a substantial independent-target moment while S13/S19 keeps its peak owned demand and completion ratio within fresh bounds? The proposed gain must survive added completion fields, changed cutoff/floor/tail offset and actual ordinary routes. This targets a large feasible mechanism, not an uncharged target-splitting fantasy.
