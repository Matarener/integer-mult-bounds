# A mathematical atlas for the next integer multiplication mechanisms

This atlas contains **95 proved informal statements**, **42 research questions**, **8 explicitly unproved source conjectures**, and **20 construction routes**. Its purpose is to give computational contributors precise mathematical mechanisms to instantiate, with proofs, interfaces, complete accounting rules and concrete acceptance witnesses. It does not claim a new funded multiplication exponent.

Start with the one-query/two-conditional-update construction in G10 and R12, the rank-r asynchronous hub in S09, the snapshot stabilizer family in G12–G15, and the class-specific and typed compiler guards in A01–A15. These change substantive interfaces or shared structure. They are more than additional decimal digits of an old root.

Dugongue, with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance from one coordinator and three parallel workers. The proofs are informal. No mathematical search, numerical pricing, source replay, solver, heavy verification or Lean formalization was run. Current repository and PR sources were read, and publication bytes were checked separately.

## What is proved and what remains a proposal

Every numbered result has an informal proof of the stated sufficient construction or accounting statement. Classical rank, module, graph, Schur, norm and convexity mathematics is credited as such. A new composition or project application is distinguished from an old theorem and from a yet-unfound physical witness. An optimistic rank, histogram or tensor equivalence is never promoted to a Gaussian supplier or to κ.

The concrete two-to-one conditional-query construction changes the ordinary alphabet by admitting noncommuting spectator flips. The asynchronous and snapshot routes change causal or owned-state structure. They do not contradict PR5676's identity-native path theorem, PR5772's fixed canonical-chart classification, PR5779's fixed helper-detour bound, or PR5626's commuting-sector minimum. Their actual changed hypothesis is stated at the corresponding route.

Cross-review covered the central new query theorem, its physical packing recipe, temporal block constructions, the sparse portfolio argument and key analytic guard updates. One missing nonnegativity assumption was corrected before release. This is informal review, not a kernel audit or a fresh source replay.
