# Precision gate: field-l2 amplification only

The displayed elementary norm products give an operator-norm amplification constant G independent of positive field width f, when each error is supplied and measured as an additive error in the Euclidean norm of the whole physical field. Signed monomials and exact coordinate Gaussians have field-l2 norm one; scalar owner operations have the stated elementary bounds.

This is not a proof that a fixed number of additional fixed-point bits suffices uniformly in f. If a field has N coordinates and each coordinate is rounded with absolute error at most epsilon, the resulting field-l2 error may be sqrt(N)*epsilon. N depends on the physical address dimension and f. The number of rounding sites, scalar-division realization and recursive Gaussian service tolerances must also be assigned under an actual coordinatewise precision/error ABI. None is removed by the constant G.

The legacy phrase finite precision offset in the mathematical MODES receipt is to be read only as a finite multiplicative tightening of an already supplied field-l2 additive-error tolerance. It is not a fixed extra-bit claim, componentwise fixed-point theorem, complete rounding proof or TM admission. The recorded logarithmic bounds are logarithms of operator norms, not certified bit-precision overheads.

The explicit rational coefficients and exact algebraic/native identities remain valid. Coordinatewise-to-field-l2 conversion, complete outer precision and bit costs, routing/storage admission and the negative E Gaussian terminal remain UNKNOWN. No exponent gain follows from the l2 constants alone.
