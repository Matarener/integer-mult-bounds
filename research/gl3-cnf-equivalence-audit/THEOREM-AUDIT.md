No symmetry, bridge, dirty-input, label-frequency or natural-representation restriction is added.

## The supported and unpruned cases have the same attaining words

Starting from identity, each paid step forms sums and a single left translation; hence every prefix coefficient lies in the radius-t address ball B_t. Write N=uvT, so N^2=N. Since the address label g is involutory and the characteristic is two, ([g]+[I])^2=0; scalar N commutes with group coefficients, and therefore F^2=I. An attaining prefix can be replayed from the final target by reversing the remaining involutions. In column c its support lies in B_(n-t) T_c, a RIGHT translate. Combining both directions gives support contained in B_t intersect (B_(n-t) T_c).

Source lines155-165 implement exactly that right translate using `product(x,target[c])`, and intersect it with the forward ball. This is the previously reviewed necessary-support argument, credited to the original source and the separate support-cone audit. Fixing coefficients outside the intersection to false removes no attaining legal word. In the reverse implication, a supported satisfying assignment still has all full transition and boundary equalities, so it directly gives an attaining word without an extra completeness assumption about pruning.

## Both directions and uniqueness

Given a satisfying assignment, selectors decode a legal word. The gate lemma converts all emitted equalities into the exact group-algebra recurrence. Starting at the fixed full identity, induction forces every represented state to the formal prefix, with outside-cone coefficients zero. The fixed final coefficients prove attainment. This proves soundness.

Given a legal attaining word, use its unique formal prefixes for all state bits and the corresponding u/v and one-hot label bits. The support lemma supplies every fixed internal zero. The boundary conditions hold, and the actual recurrence satisfies every equality. Complete bidirectional helper clauses have their unique gate completion. This proves completeness. Prefixes are uniquely determined from fixed selectors and initial state, so internal states and then auxiliaries are also unique.
