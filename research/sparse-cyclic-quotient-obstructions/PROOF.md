# Sharp obstructions to sparse cyclic compression

Let S be a finite subset of Z^d. A cyclic projection is a homomorphism

    phi_w,q(x) = w dot x modulo q,       q >= 2, w in Z^d.

Call it faithful on S when its restriction to S is injective. The ambient
lattice Z^d is fixed. We optimize over all integer weights; weights need
not be units. This concerns a certificate's exponent support, not a physical
address operation.

## Two-point supports

**Theorem 1.** Let S={u,v}, u != v, and let h>0 be the gcd of the coordinates
of v-u. A faithful projection to Z/q exists if and only if q does not divide h.
Consequently its smallest possible modulus is

    mu(h) = min { q >= 2 : q does not divide h }.

**Proof.** If q divides every coordinate difference, every dot product
vanishes modulo q. Conversely, if q does not divide h, it fails to divide
at least one coordinate difference. The corresponding coordinate weight
vector separates the two points. The gcd characterization follows because
q divides h exactly when it divides every coordinate difference. Translation
only adds a constant residue and does not affect injectivity. QED.

**Corollary 1.** Every mu(h) is a prime power, and every prime power occurs.
No bound depending only on |S| or d bounds a faithful modulus, even for
|S|=2 and d=1.

**Proof.** Put m=mu(h). Every positive integer below m divides h. If m has
at least two distinct prime factors, all its maximal prime-power factors
are below m and divide h. Their least common multiple is m, a contradiction.
Thus m is a prime power. Conversely, for a prime power m=p^a, set
h=lcm(1,...,m-1). Its p-adic valuation is a-1, so m does not divide h, while
every smaller positive integer does. Thus mu(h)=m. Arbitrarily large prime
powers give the unboundedness. QED.

This is an existence minimum, not merely a failure of one weight heuristic.
The support points remain distinct over the integers throughout.
The fixed ambient lattice matters. If the allowed domain instead becomes
the subgroup generated by the differences, then {0,h} in hZ maps faithfully
to Z/2 by sending h to one. That map need not extend to the original Z.
The unboundedness therefore does not obstruct a frontend that first rebases
the generated subgroup. It is a precise limitation of integer weights on
the supplied coordinates.

**Corollary 2.** For integer H>=1, the sharp worst-case minimum among
two-point differences with maximum absolute coordinate at most H is

    max mu(h) = min { m >= 2 : lcm(1,...,m) > H }.

**Proof.** Write L_m=lcm(1,...,m), and let m be the first index with L_m>H.
For any 1<=h<=H, the supposition mu(h)>m implies L_m divides h,
contradicting L_m>H. Thus mu(h)<=m. The scalar difference h=L_(m-1)
is at most H. Also m does not divide it, since otherwise L_m=L_(m-1)<=H.
Every smaller integer divides it, so this difference attains mu(h)=m.
Every nonzero vector difference in the stated box has gcd at most H, and
the scalar witness embeds into any positive dimension. QED.

For example, S={0,6} has optimal modulus four. Restricting the search to
prime moduli can therefore miss the optimum.

## Pairwise possibility does not give a common projection

**Theorem 2.** The support

    S = {(0,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,1), (2,2)}

has minimum faithful cyclic modulus six. At modulus five, every pair admits
a separating weight vector, but no weight vector separates all five points.
The mixed-radix box bound is nine.

**Proof.** Cardinality excludes moduli below five. Suppose weights (a,b)
are injective modulo five. The first four points have images 0,a,b,a+b.
Their distinctness forces a,b != 0 and b/a not in {0,1,-1}. Over F5 the
remaining possibilities are b/a=2 or 3. Scale by a^-1. If b/a=2, the final
point has image 2(1+2)=1, colliding with (1,0). If b/a=3, it has image
2(1+3)=3, colliding with (0,1). Thus modulus five is impossible.

For every pair, the coordinate difference is nonzero modulo five. At least
one coordinate projection separates it, proving the pairwise assertion.
At modulus six, weights (1,3) give the distinct residues (0,1,3,4,2),
in the displayed order. The coordinate widths are both two, so the box
construction gives (2+1)(2+1)=9. QED.

The first four points are exactly the subset sums of paid labels
(1,0),(0,1); the last point can be a target exponent. Thus this is a
subset-sum-plus-target support of the predecessor's declared form. It can
be used at any register count. No candidate word or true target identity
is asserted merely from this support description.
Here the support generates all of Z^2, so rebasing its generated subgroup
cannot avoid the optimum. All homomorphisms out of that group are already
among the integer weights considered above.

## Consequence for finite replay

PR #1440 supplies the sufficient mixed-radix projection and faithful
regular-action criterion. We use that criterion without replaying its
fixtures or claiming it as new. For completeness, a binary formal coefficient
vector supported on S maps to a cyclic coefficient vector by XORing terms
with equal residues. If the projection is injective, different support terms
cannot cancel, so this coefficient map is injective. If two points collide,
their two-term sum is a nonzero formal polynomial with zero cyclic image.
The regular action recovers cyclic coefficients by acting on the basis
vector at address zero. Checking every role/address column detects exactly
the cyclic coefficient map, including its kernel at an unfaithful projection.

The new fixture has 32 binary support polynomials. The checker enumerates
them all, with all weights modulo one through six, and compares direct
translated address columns with coefficient convolution. Each noninjective
weight gets an actual two-term kernel witness. Modulus one is a rejection
control, not an allowed optimum. The written proofs establish the unbounded
statements; finite checks do not extrapolate them.

This is not an error in PR #1440 or an obstruction to its valid box bound.
It explains why a smaller exact quotient needs arithmetic information and
a simultaneous injection check. Native realization, physical conversion,
complete signed moment, supplier admission and the multiplication theorem
remain outside this result.
