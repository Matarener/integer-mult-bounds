# Rigidity of Gaussian lattices under local Clifford generators

This proof was produced by Nick Petalas. Apache-2.0.

Write O=Z[i], pi=1+i, r>=1 and N=2^r. Let B_r be the Gaussian Newton lattice
from Clear1nsight's PR3382, `research/p104-clifford-exactness`:

    B_r = { f(x)=sum_S a_S x_S : pi^|S| divides a_S },
    E_r = [[1,0],[1,pi]] tensor ... tensor [[1,0],[1,pi]],
    B_r = E_r O^N.

We cite its all-rank maximality and intertwiners. In particular, every integral
Gaussian module invariant under every local ideal butterfly C_j lies inside
B_r. The local physical operators are

    C(a,b)=((a+b+i(a-b))/2, (a+b-i(a-b))/2),
    S(a,b)=(a,ib).

In E_1 coefficient coordinates their matrices are

    A = [[1,1],[0,i]],       B = [[1,0],[i,i]].

The predecessor proves that B_r is stable under these and the broader affine
address/Clifford monomial alphabet, and that the literal signed-floor word is
exact on B_r. Those exactness/closure theorems are not new claims here.

## 1. The generated integral order is the whole matrix ring

Let E_ab denote an ordinary two-by-two matrix unit, with zero-based indices.
Exact multiplication gives

    (A-iI)(B-iI) = -i E_00.

Indeed the only possibly nonzero entry is (0,0), whose value is
`(1-i)^2+i=-i`, a Gaussian unit. Therefore the unital O-algebra generated by
A and B contains

    E_00 = i (A-iI)(B-iI),
    E_01 = E_00 A - E_00,
    E_10 = -i (B E_00 - E_00),
    E_11 = I-E_00.

These are all matrix units. Conversely A and B have entries in O. Hence

    O[A,B] = M_2(O).                                             (1)

At r bits, the generators at bit j act as A or B at that tensor factor and
identity at all others. Each local algebra thus contains its four local matrix
units. Their products over the r distinct factors are every global matrix unit
E_uv, with u,v in {0,1}^r. It follows that

    O[A_1,B_1,...,A_r,B_r] = M_N(O).                             (2)

Here the subscripted B_j denotes a coefficient phase matrix, not the Newton
lattice. The argument uses both local generators on every bit; omitting a
phase can invalidate the conclusion.

## 2. Classification of all invariant integral Gaussian modules

**Theorem.** Every nonzero O-submodule L of O^N invariant under all the
physical C_j and S_j is of the form

    L = alpha B_r,             0 != alpha in Z[i].               (3)

The scalar is unique up to a Gaussian unit. Conversely every such scalar
multiple is invariant. In particular there is no further nonhomothetic
integral lattice in this fixed alphabet.

**Proof.** By the credited maximality theorem, L is contained in B_r. Thus
Lambda=E_r^-1 L is an O-submodule of O^N. It is stable under all coefficient
generators, and hence under their full matrix algebra (2).

Put J={a in O : a e_0 belongs to Lambda}. This is an ideal. If v belongs
to Lambda, then E_0j v=v_j e_0 belongs to Lambda, so each v_j belongs to J.
Therefore Lambda is contained in J^N. Conversely a e_0 in Lambda implies
a e_j=E_j0(a e_0) in Lambda for every j. Addition gives J^N contained in
Lambda. Thus Lambda=J^N.

The Gaussian integers form a principal ideal domain, so J=alpha O. Since
Lambda is nonzero, alpha is nonzero. Then L=E_r(alpha O^N)=alpha B_r.
Equality of principal ideals gives uniqueness up to units. The converse
follows from the predecessor's invariant O-module B_r. This also shows that
any nonzero module in scope has full rank and finite index; these need not
be additional hypotheses. The zero module is the sole zero case.

If desired, the index follows from the known Newton determinant:

    [O^N : alpha B_r] = (alpha*conjugate(alpha))^N
                        * 2^(r 2^(r-1)).                        (4)

The index of B_r itself is predecessor/classical tensor-lattice arithmetic.
The new statement is the absence of any other invariant module shape.

The hypothesis of Gaussian-module closure matters: it includes arbitrary
Gaussian integer linear combinations, or equivalently additive closure plus
the global i phase. It does not cover correlated, nonlinear or nonadditive
entrance domains. Requiring each local C_j and S_j is stronger than requiring
one particular whole transform and its inverse.

## 3. Uniform integral quarter closure is impossible in this class

**Corollary.** No nonzero L in the theorem satisfies

    f in L and f/4 in O^N  =>  f/4 in L.                         (5)

This is a closure requirement on all admitted independent sources, not a
claim that every actual quarter operation fails.

**Proof.** Write L=alpha B_r and m=max(r,4). On the binary cube set

    f(x)=alpha pi^m x_1...x_r.

Its sole nonzero Newton coefficient is divisible by alpha pi^r, so f belongs
to L. Every coordinate is divisible by four because pi^4=-4 and m>=4.
The quotient f/4 is consequently a Gaussian-integer field.

After cancelling the nonzero alpha, its top Newton coefficient has pi
valuation m-4. This is strictly below r for every r>=1. Thus f/4 is outside
alpha B_r, contradicting (5). This is a constructive witness for every alpha
and r, not a finite failed search.

Consequently a uniform replacement lattice with the same independent-input
and local-generator contract cannot repair the quarter-ADD boundary merely
by choosing another invariant lattice. A source in 4L can still be divided
into L. A caller can therefore change the boundary to a divisibility
filtration, restrict the source updates, change the gate alphabet, or prove
a correlated/nonlinear entrance. These alternatives require their own proof
and complete cost; the corollary excludes none of them.

## 4. Finite corroboration and scope

The verifier constructs the four matrix units from the displayed A,B words
and checks all sixteen products. For ranks one through seven it tensors the
units and checks the action of every resulting E_uv on a dense coefficient
vector with distinct nonzero Gaussian entries. The two-by-two matrix equality
and written tensor argument establish the operator identity; a dense test
alone would not prove it.

A separate complete finite module census enumerates all 67 additive subgroups
of (Z[i]/2)^2, imposes scalar i and A,B invariance, and finds exactly the
three scalar-ideal modules: zero, (pi)/(2) in each coordinate, and the whole
module. This checks the first nontrivial ideal sandwich. It does not prove
classification at arbitrary modulus or dimension by enumeration.

An explicit C-only skew lattice in coefficient coordinates, O e_0 + pi O e_1,
is A-invariant but not B-invariant. Signed fixtures check scalar-ideal
preservation; the universal quarter witness is tested for seven scalars at
each rank through seven. No predecessor scientific replay is performed.

This is a scoped written classification and quarter-closure obstruction, with
finite exact corroboration. No complete caller, physical/native admission,
precision/tape or recursive cost improvement, new kappa, complete multiplication
theorem, independent human review or formal proof-kernel acceptance follows.
Matrix-unit arguments and the ideal classification for full matrix rings are
classical; the new application is the full integral order and resulting rigidity
of this fixed P104 entrance alphabet.
