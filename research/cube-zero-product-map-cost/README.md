# Exact cost from the cube-zero product map

This package answers the product-map question in Nick Petalas's PR4764. For the
same two-role outer-product target, it classifies exactly when quadratic
relations lower the paid right-vector cost from three to two. It also gives the
minimum dimension of the product image needed for a cost-three witness. The
complete kappa calculation is unchanged.

## Theorem

Let A=K plus J be a split commutative local algebra over a field K, with J cubed
zero. Choose x,y whose images span a two-dimensional subspace U of J/J squared.
Let mu: Sym squared(U) -> J squared be multiplication and let L=ker(mu).
Identify Sym squared(U) with the three-dimensional binary quadratic forms
on formal variables X,Y. Use paid factors I+u v^T, u in A squared and v in
K squared, and arbitrary free GL2(K) maps. Singular paid factors are allowed.
Put M=[[x squared,xy],[xy,y squared]]. Then

```
cost(M) = 3  if L contains no nonzero product of two K-linear forms;
cost(M) = 2  otherwise.
```

Equivalently, cost is three exactly when L is zero or is a line generated by
an irreducible binary quadratic. This covers every field, including
characteristic two and inseparable irreducible quadratics. It classifies this
outer-product family, not all matrices over a cube-zero algebra.

## Proof

The predecessor's three paid factors work unchanged:

```
C1 = [[x,0],[y,1]], C2 = [[1,y],[0,0]], C3 = [[x,0],[0,1]],
C1 C2 C3 = M.
```

Each factor changes one base-field column direction. The identity uses only
commutativity. All entries of M lie in J squared. The exact two-factor criterion
of PR4276 says that cost is at most two exactly when some N=G M H, with G,H
in GL2(K), satisfies N12 in N11 A. Since J cubed is zero, N11 A=K N11.
Thus this condition is equivalent to a nonzero row phi and column v over K
with phi M v=0. The forward direction subtracts the relevant scalar multiple
of the first column of H from its second. The reverse direction chooses phi
as the first row of G and v as the second column of H.

For phi=(a,b) and v=(c,d), phi M v=(ax+by)(cx+dy). This is zero exactly when
the nonzero split form (aX+bY)(cX+dY) lies in L. If such a pair exists, choose
G,H as above; then N12=0 and the explicit two-factor construction is

```
N = [[N11,0],[N21,1]] [[1,0],[0,N22]],
M = G^-1 N H^-1.
```

No zero- or one-factor word can equal M. Its scalar residue is the zero
2-by-2 matrix. A free map has rank two, and the scalar residue of I+u v^T
has rank at least one. Invertible free maps do not change that lower bound.
This includes M=0: the zero matrix has cost two in this alphabet.

It remains to classify L. If dim(L)>=2, the linear functional that takes the
X squared coefficient has a nonzero kernel on L. A nonzero form there is
Y(bX+cY), hence split. A one-dimensional L avoids all split forms exactly
when its generator is irreducible. The zero space also avoids them. This
proves the classification without a census or a field-size restriction.

## Minimum product image and finite-field count

The rank of mu is three minus dim(L). A cost-three witness therefore needs
at least two product directions. Rank two suffices exactly when K has an
irreducible quadratic. For such a form Q, the five-dimensional algebra
K[X,Y]/((X,Y) cubed,Q) attains rank two and cost three. If K has no irreducible
quadratic, rank three is necessary and K[X,Y]/(X,Y) cubed attains it.
The minimum concerns this two-plane witness. It is not a minimum dimension
for every possible right-vector obstruction or every local algebra.

Over F_q, there are 1+q(q-1)/2 relation subspaces with cost three: the zero
space and the irreducible quadratic lines. A nonzero irreducible binary
quadratic has nonzero leading coefficient and gives one monic irreducible
quadratic. Of the q squared monic quadratics, q(q+1)/2 are products with an
unordered pair of roots in F_q. This gives the stated count. All other
subspaces give cost two. The total number of subspaces of F_q cubed is
2q squared+2q+4. Extra unused directions in J squared do not change the cost.

For F2 the minimal example imposes X squared+XY+Y squared=0. Its product
image has dimension two, and every pair of nonzero forms on U has nonzero
product. Thus the predecessor's six-dimensional coefficient algebra is not
minimal for this witness.

## Evidence and reproduction

```sh
python3.12 -B scripts/research_package.py --package research/cube-zero-product-map-cost
```

Use Python 3.12 and the standard library. The verifier enumerates every
quadratic relation subspace over F2,F3,F5,F7. It compares literal products of
all projective pairs with a separate polynomial-root test, checks the full
three-factor word, and constructs a two-factor free chart in every low-cost
case. It applies each word to all independent quotient-basis input columns.
Controls reject reversed order, deleted factors and a split relation mistaken
for an irreducible one. Normal and optimized replay use explicit exceptions.
The written proof supplies the all-field claim. No extension-field census,
predecessor scientific replay, Lean or selected cold replay is claimed.

## Open next questions

Find a physical realization of the five-dimensional irreducible-quotient
algebra and price each coefficient operation in the three-factor word. Start
with the explicit F2 relation above. Coefficient preparation and the complete
producer/consumer cost are the blockers; fewer coefficient dimensions alone
do not give a recursive saving. A different two-plane in a specified larger
cube-zero algebra can be tested directly by the proved kernel criterion.
