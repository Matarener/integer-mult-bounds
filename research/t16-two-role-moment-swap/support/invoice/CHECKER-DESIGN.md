# Invoice reviewer design and limits

`review_complete_invoice.py` is an accounting and source-binding check. It imports no producer, placement, moment or outer module. It does not replace worker4's construction gate or worker3's independent arithmetic.

It compares the complete selected invoice instead of maintaining a partial allowlist of paid costs. Only template/owner/controller identity hashes may differ. The checker resolves those hashes to the actual new bytes. Every other nested field, including future invoice fields, must match exactly. It separately connects the changed recursive profile, actual chart count and all strict/scorer receipts to the nominated three data files.

The actual14826 and conservative14850 physical counts are deliberately distinct. Their exact Clifford-group stock bitlengths agree at2642, so both imply the same paid row16469. The reviewer does not change the frozen independent checker or claim a storage saving.

Nine behavior controls remove a paid primitive/coefficient/wrapper/allowance/prefix charge, add an unknown invoice field, omit cap coverage, remove the bit surcharge, or weaken scope. Four more controls mix scorer source, assembly, owner-file or profile revisions. All13 reject. A live development check also caught a score report naming an older scorer source after the file changed. The accepted report uses only worker3's final immutable support-candidate-001-v3 source/report, with direct six-input and assembly binding.

## Engineering checks

Build the Lever led to a rerunnable checker rather than a prose-only invoice claim. Prove It Works led to checking the emitted invoice, stage logs and source bytes directly. Power of Ten led to bounded reads, short functions and fail-closed validation. All functions are shorter than60 physical lines; loops are bounded by canonical invoice/source inventories, six cap decisions, two47-slack paths, two7-step bootstrap chains, eleven stages, thirteen controls and the fixed36-factor group calculation. No new recursion, subprocess or shared mutable global state is introduced.

Managed Python objects and JSON/gzip parsing allocate dynamically. Direct fixed-capacity allocation is not available for those standard-library operations. The compensating bounds are2MiB per JSON input,32MiB for hashed artifacts and128MiB expanded template payload, plus byte-authenticated canonical baseline archives and fixed expected object shapes. Malformed or exhausted input fails rather than producing acceptance. This batch checker has no steady-state service. A future untrusted-file service would need stricter depth/node quotas and process memory isolation before reuse.
