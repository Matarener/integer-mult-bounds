# Two subspace-cut modules cannot close the S4 tariff gap

Fix `T=(2143,3142,4231,2143)` and adjacent generators `s1=2134`,
`s2=1324`, `s3=1243` in S4. Permutations use one-line notation; products
act rightmost first. This is a proof-method obstruction, not a circuit optimum.

## Cut contract

For a representation rho on V over a field K and a subspace U put

    c_U(g) = dim(U) - dim(U intersect rho(g)U).

This is the rank of `U -> V/U` induced by rho(g). It is at most
`min(dim(U),dim(V)-dim(U))`. The equal-dimension subspace distance satisfies
the triangle inequality, so `c_U(gh)<=c_U(g)+c_U(h)`.

For application to a binary formal word, K must have characteristic two.
Then `F2[S4]` maps into the representation. On R scalar roles, repeat U as
`K^R tensor U`. A paid factor `I+N tensor(rho(s_i)-I)`, with binary rank-one
idempotent N, has crossing rank c_U(s_i). Free scalar mixing has crossing
rank zero. The off-diagonal block formula for a product gives crossing-rank
subadditivity. A diagonal endpoint with targets T and any number of restored
dirty roles has crossing rank `sum_T c_U(t)`. Thus nonnegative combinations
of these cuts give valid bounds when their generator charges fit the prices.
Every dirty role is included and has its full identity endpoint prescribed.

The natural module is the coordinate permutation action on K^4. The binary
edge module is F2^6 with basis the six unordered pairs of four points.
Every cut below uses one module copy uniformly over all roles. Arbitrary
subspaces coupling multiple copies and larger representations are excluded.

## Result

Every natural-module subspace over every field, and every binary edge-module
subspace, satisfies

    sum_T c_U(t) <= 4 c_U(s1)+2 c_U(s2)+4 c_U(s3).              (1)

Consequently the cone generated by these valid binary-word cuts has exact
optimum `4 p1+2 p2+4 p3` at every nonnegative price vector `(p1,p2,p3)`.
This includes natural cuts over arbitrary characteristic-two extension fields.
At `(2,1,2)` the optimum is 18. Adding every binary edge cut cannot improve it.

Adding this class to PR2998's entire coordinate-cut class on finite permutation
S4-modules leaves the exact bound `8 beta+4 alpha` at `(beta,alpha,beta)`,
`0<=alpha<=beta`. At `(2,1,2)` this is 20, against separate scalar cost 21.
The actual arbitrary-dirty-role circuit optimum remains 20 or 21.

The all-field natural inequality is an algebraic statement. Odd-characteristic
representations are not homomorphic images of the binary group algebra and
are not silently admitted as binary-word certificates.

## Natural-module proof over every field

Write `(a,b,c)=(c_U(s1),c_U(s2),c_U(s3))`. Each generator has displacement
rank one, so these values belong to `{0,1}`. Every cut in dimension four
is at most two. The actual identities

    2143 = s1 s3,
    3142 = s2 s1 s3,
    4231 = s1 s2 s3 s2 s1
         = s3 s2 s1 s2 s3
         = s1 s3 s2 s3 s1

bound the tuple crossing sum by

    2 min(2,a+c)+min(2,a+b+c)
      + min(2,2a+2b+c,a+2b+2c,2a+b+2c).

In lexicographic binary order the eight bounds are `0,4,2,6,4,8,6,8`.
Each is at most `4a+2b+4c`. This proves (1) over every field, including
infinite fields, without extrapolating from finite-field samples.

## Complete binary edge proof

F2^6 has exactly 2,825 subspaces, with dimension counts
`1,63,651,1395,651,63,1`. The primary checker starts at `{0}` and repeatedly
takes the span with each vector outside the current space. Every subspace
has a basis and therefore a chain of such extensions. Exact membership-set
deduplication gives complete coverage. For each of the seven actual
permutations, exact intersection cardinality gives `dim(U intersect gU)`.
Every resulting profile satisfies (1). No heuristic pruning, floating-point
acceptance or timeout exclusion is used.

The second checker enumerates reduced row-echelon bases by pivot positions
and every allowed free entry. Every subspace has exactly one such basis.
It computes each cut as `rank(U+gU)-dim(U)` by dense elimination and imports
no primary code. Both checkers agree on the entire membership-set SHA256,
every seven-permutation cut profile and its multiplicity, and the full slack
histogram in RESULT.json. These are separately implemented author checks,
not independent human or proof-kernel verification.

The same comparison covers all 67 binary natural subspaces. The second
checker also covers all 212 natural subspaces over F3 and 1,120 over F5.
These corroborate the all-field lemma and do not supply binary-word cuts.

## Sharp module cone

The natural coordinate prefix spaces of dimensions one, two and three have
generator profiles `(1,0,0)`, `(0,1,0)`, `(0,0,1)` and tuple sums `4,2,4`.
Giving them weights p1,p2,p3 attains `4 p1+2 p2+4 p3`. Conversely, multiply
(1) by each nonnegative cut weight, add, and use the three price constraints.
This proves the matching upper bound, including convergent nonnegative sums.

## Enlarged cone

Retain PR2998's credited pentagonal argument. A coordinate-cut profile is
half a symmetric-difference distance. The pentagonal inequality on

    x=(1234,2143,2413), y=(1243,2134)

bounds the tuple sum by `2 c(s1)+2 c(s3)+c(2431)+c(4213)`. Both last
permutations have words using each exterior generator once and the middle
generator twice. Hence every old coordinate cut satisfies

    sum_T c(t) <= 4(c(s1)+c(s2)+c(s3)).                        (2)

This is the retained theorem, not a new coordinate-cone proof. Equation (1)
implies (2) for all new module cuts. Their union therefore has the common
dual `(4,4,4)`, whose objective is `8 beta+4 alpha`.

The inherited ordered-pair cut has profile inv(g), charges one on each
generator and totals twelve on T. The two exterior prefix cuts together
have charges `(1,0,1)` and total eight. Weights alpha and beta-alpha attain
`12 alpha+8(beta-alpha)=8 beta+4 alpha`. They are nonnegative in exactly
the stated price cone. Thus the union has that exact optimum. The inherited
separate scalar bill `9 beta+3 alpha` remains a different quantity.
