# Primary literature consulted

These sources informed questions and proof organization. None certifies the
P104 ownership, native operations or recursive tariff used here.

- Bostan, Lecerf and Schost, *Tellegen's Principle into Practice*, ISSAC 2003
  ([author-hosted paper](https://mathexp.eu/bostan/publications/BoLeSc03.pdf)).
  Linear-program transposition helps inspect how local errors reach outputs.
  The repository already has an owned transpose construction; generic
  transposition is not claimed as new here.
- Schaeffer and Perkowski, *A Cost Minimization Approach to Synthesis of Linear
  Reversible Circuits* ([primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.0070)).
  Bidirectional synthesis motivates considering both prefix and suffix maps.
  Our condition `S(R-T)P=0` and its full-rank consequence are elementary linear
  algebra, independently proved here; no P104 cost theorem comes from the paper.
- Paradis, Bichsel and Vechev, *Reqomp: Space-constrained Uncomputation for Quantum
  Circuits*, Quantum 8, 1258 (2024)
  ([primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10395)). Value-version scheduling
  motivates earlier equivalent producers or changed lifetimes. The loss-space
  invariant tests unchanged scalar values. P104's inherited owned CLEAR can
  erase a nonzero field, so quantum uncomputation costs cannot replace this
  source's ownership contract.

A genuinely different next test is coupled synthesis of a larger public+owned
window containing the public contact that causes a conflict and at least two
lifetimes. Allow intermediate public bases and payloads to change while
requiring the exact external map, then price the complete signed recursive
invoice. This is our proposed application of literature ideas, not a published
P104 result. Reject a candidate before full pricing if its altered dependency
cannot preserve external reads. None of these references establishes that
such a candidate exists or improves the bound.
