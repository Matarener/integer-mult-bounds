## 6. Counterexamples actively checked and their boundaries

The following would defeat broader readings, but do not defeat the sealed lemma:

1. **Scalar-scaled ordinary targets.** If h=2g with g in G, then h may lie outside G while `(2g)^(tensor f)` is ordinary in the enlarged grammar. Zero paid calls suffice. The lemma correctly strengthens its target test to h outside SG. The retained nonmonomial h_i cannot be a scalar multiple of a monomial, so they pass that stronger test.
2. **Sparse width subsets.** `[−I]−[I]` vanishes on every even width although it is nonzero formally. All positive widths or a consecutive tail is therefore substantive. A source offering only selected widths has no automatic transfer.
3. **Arbitrary width-dependent scalars.** Allowing general c(f), width-dependent exponents n(f), or a word length growing with f defeats the fixed finite Laurent expansion used before Vandermonde. These remain outside scope. The extension is specifically c·2^(nf) with fixed c,n in a fixed finite word.
4. **Width-one Gaussian expansion.** A one-column equality `Q=aI+bX` does not imply the all-width group-algebra identity `[Q]=a[I]+b[X]`. Scalar powers of two do not make that substitution valid.
5. **Localization poles.** Formal fractions cannot be interpreted as free pointwise scalar instructions. The proof does not do so, and no pole width is discarded from the endpoint promise.
6. **Paid-row closure.** A noncommuting paid call cannot apply to every ordinary transform of an earlier response for one charge. Only its actual row is adjoined here.
7. **Paid/cached input history.** If correctly versioned h_i w_vi rows are supplied from earlier paid work, zero new calls can satisfy these outputs. The standalone ordinary-input-history premise is retained. Surjectivity alone does not remove correlated side information.
