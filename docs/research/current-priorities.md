## Price the current contract

Write `T_r` for productive targets and `P_r` for paid children. At a candidate
saving `C`, compare the full target and paid power sums with exponent `1-C`,
including every additional fee required by that supplier's normalization.
A favorable raw margin is only the first gate: finite compiler closure,
native operators, dirty restoration, stock/precision and outer use still need
coverage. Productive helpers are not free zero-initialized scratch.

Use [signed invoice preflight](recursive-invoice-preflight.md) when old and new
boundary contracts match. A concave-order certificate can settle a full family
of tariffs; an inconclusive result is not a proof of a power crossing. The new
selected-invoice exporter (#5735) reconstructs the authenticated P104 ledger;
#5732 bounds a fixed rational power and #5746 handles common-base ranks over
the whole power interval. These are accounting tools, not native or compiler
proofs. Changed
contracts need a justified comparison, not a silent reuse of the old bill.

For **κ > .01**, work backward from the actual nine outer rows and both suppliers
at that target, with strict margins. The present .0034 admitted BIT budget cannot
support it. Specify a mechanism capable of a favorable fully charged target-scale
bill before expanding the search. A hypothetical passing profile, such as #5614's
145-split benchmark, does not supply the physical word. No current result proves
a universal ceiling for finite networks.

Old .001687 packing caps, 64-per-call centre costs and ideal lever multipliers
remain historical evidence in earlier priority-log entries. They must not be used
as current limits without proving exact model/inventory equivalence. In the same
way, the thirteen-helper refinement bound in #5702 concerns that fixed expansion,
not every target split. New topology, changed data paths or a different recursive
boundary require a fresh complete budget.
